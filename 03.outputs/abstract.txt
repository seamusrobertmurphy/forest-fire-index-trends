In 2025, Canada revised the equations and weather input of its Fire Weather
Index System. Published fire danger trends had been computed under earlier
versions, as had the fixed index values at which agencies rated danger high or
extreme. No study had tested whether these revisions changed those trends. This
study used hourly records from five stations in the Okanagan basin, British
Columbia, each holding 6 to 27 complete fire seasons between 1991 and 2025. Daily
indices were computed under both versions from the 13:00 local time observation
and under the 2025 version from hourly weather, giving three series. Trends in
twelve metrics were estimated by Theil-Sen slope and Mann-Kendall test with Hamed
and Rao's autocorrelation correction, and slope differences by moving-block
bootstrap. Of 144 trends, 140 were positive and 35 passed false discovery rate
correction, 33 of them at one station. No slope difference between series excluded zero, so the revision changed no trend detectably. Instead, days per season above a Fine
Fuel Moisture Code of 91 rose from 57 to 62 when the 2025 version ran on hourly
weather. Combining the two versions in one record would thus add 4.7 days caused
by the revision rather than the weather. Under the 2025 version, days above a
Fire Weather Index of 19 stayed at 78 on hourly weather but rose to 85 on the 13:00
observation. Fire danger trends could therefore be compared between the two
versions, but counts of days above a fixed index value could not.
