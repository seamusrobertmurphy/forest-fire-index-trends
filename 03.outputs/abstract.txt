The 2025 update to the Canadian Forest Fire Danger Rating System recalculated
its three fuel moisture codes hourly, with faster litter drying and per-event
rain thresholds. It also limited the Initial Spread Index wind effect above 40
kilometres per hour. The weather input changed from daily to hourly. No study
had separated the two changes or tested trends across both. This study
computed three series from one hourly record of 104 station-seasons at five
Okanagan basin stations, British Columbia, 1991 to 2025. The 1984 version used the
13:00 observation, the 2025 version that observation and hourly weather, and
twelve metrics covered every component. Trends were estimated by Theil-Sen slope
and Mann-Kendall test with the Hamed and Rao autocorrelation correction, slope
differences by moving-block bootstrap, and lightning ignitions by negative
binomial regression. Of 144 trends, 140 were positive and 35 passed the test,
33 at one station. Between versions, levels moved and trends did not. At fixed input, the 2025 equations raised the mean Duff Moisture Code by 5.8 and the peak Drought Code by 60, towards drier fuel.
Across both changes a Fine Fuel Moisture Code of 91 fell from percentile 65.1 to
62.5, so 62 rather than 57 days per season exceeded it. None of 24 slope
differences under fixed or percentile thresholds excluded zero, at a
resolution of 16.8 days, and lightning ignition skill differed by 0.0005 in log
likelihood. Trends should be reported as days above a percentile threshold,
with the version stated.
