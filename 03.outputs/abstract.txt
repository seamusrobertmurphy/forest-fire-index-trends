In 2025 the Canadian Forest Fire Weather Index System, a process model of fuel
moisture and fire behaviour, changed its calculation and weather input, from
daily to hourly. In the Okanagan basin of British Columbia trends were
comparable across that change as days above a percentile threshold and not
above a fixed value. Almost every published Canadian trend was in the 1987
units, and no study had separated the two changes. This study computed three
series from one hourly weather record (5 stations, 104 station-seasons, 31
seasons, 1991 to 2025), FWI1987, the 1987 version, on the 13:00 observation and
FWI2025 on that observation and hourly weather. Trends were estimated by
Theil-Sen slope and Mann-Kendall test with the Hamed and Rao autocorrelation
correction, series differences by bootstrap over blocks of seasons, and daily
ignitions by negative binomial regression with season fixed effects. The
version change moved the level; a Fine Fuel Moisture Code of 91 was at
percentiles 65.1 and 62.5 of the 1987 and hourly series, 57 against 62 days
per season. Of 12 between-version slope differences under fixed thresholds, 0
excluded zero, against 0 of 8 under percentile thresholds, at a resolution
of about 16.8 days over the window, refuting the predicted slope divergence. On
held-out seasons the three series differed by 0.0005 in mean lightning ignition
log likelihood per day, with FWI1987 on the 13:00 observation ranking first. Trends should therefore be reported as days above a
percentile threshold, with the version stated.
