Question Q3, whether the daily probability of fire has been tested for change over time in Canada and related to fire danger anomalies.

Establishes Woolford, Dean, Martell, Cao and Wotton modelled the daily probability that one or more lightning fires were reported in two northwestern Ontario ecoregions from 1963 to 2009 with a mixture model carrying temporal trend terms, found the probability rising, and related the rise to temperature and fire danger index anomalies. The full text sits behind the Wiley paywall and the publisher blocked retrieval on 2026-10-13, so only the abstract from the Crossref record was read, and the model form, the seasonal terms and the test statistics could not be quoted. Environmetrics 25(6), 406 to 416.

Quotations

"We develop a flexible mixture-modelling framework that permits the joint assessment of temporal trends in these dominant characteristics in terms of fire risk, defined as the daily probability that one or more fires are reported." (abstract)

"We identify statistically significant increases in lightning-caused fire risk between 1963 and 2009 in the boreal forest regions of the Rainy River and Lake of the Woods ecoregions in Northwestern Ontario, Canada." (abstract)

"These observed changes in lightning-caused fire risk were found to be associated with temperature and fire danger rating index anomalies." (abstract)

"If such trends continue into the future, the duration of elevated periods of lightning-caused forest fire risk is forecasted to increase by over 50% by the middle of this century." (abstract)
