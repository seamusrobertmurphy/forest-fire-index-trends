Question Q3, because it modelled a 41 year monthly record of lightning ignition counts against observed fire danger in a Gaussian Bayesian network after transforming the negative binomial counts to normal scores, assessed skill by rolling origin evaluation with a twelve month horizon, and checked the stability of the fitted structure over three data splits as its test of stationarity.

Establishes Bates, Dowdy and McCaw, Climate Dynamics 57, 1207 to 1225, built a sparse dynamic Bayesian network in the R package bnlearn, learned by tabu search on the Bayesian Gaussian equivalent score with 500 bootstrap replications for arc strength, linking six climate mode indices at a one month lag, the monthly McArthur Mark V Forest Fire Danger Index from the gridded analysis of Dowdy (2018), prescribed burn area and lightning ignition counts for the 1.6 million ha Warren region of southwest Australia from April 1976 to December 2016, 489 months of which 95 had non zero counts. The counts followed a negative binomial distribution with mean 0.759 and shape 0.100, but the network required Gaussian nodes, so every variable was converted to a standard normal score by the randomised probability integral transform of Dunn and Smyth (1996), and no month or harmonic term entered because the lag one structure carried the seasonality. The fitted graph put the Forest Fire Danger Index at time t as the only parent of the ignition count, with no direct arc from any climate mode, so the climate modes acted on ignitions through fire weather alone and a reference network with local variables only gave identical ignition predictions. Rolling origin evaluation with 351 training months and a 12 month horizon over 138 test months gave a cross validated coefficient of determination of 0.74 for the fire danger index against 0.63 for the reference network, while both networks underpredicted ignition counts above 5. The authors assumed the structure time invariant, tested it only by comparing graphs fitted to 70, 85 and 100 per cent of the record, found one Indian Ocean Dipole to Niño 3.4 arc strengthening from 0.50 to 0.90 across the splits, and did not fit a count regression, a Poisson or negative binomial likelihood, or a trend in the danger to occurrence link.

"Indices for six climate variability modes are considered along with two hazard variables (observed fire weather conditions and prescribed burn area), and a 41-year record of lightning-ignited wildfire counts." (p. 1207)

"For the intended purposes of this study we make the following assumptions for our sDBN: (1) the network structure and the probability distributions and associated parameters describing the temporal dependencies are time invariant (while noting that the temporal patterns of residuals from the models are examined for trend); (2) the joint probability distribution of the random variables is Gaussian; (3) the DAG is not required to be fully connected, and (4) the system behaves like a low-order autoregressive process (i.e., the current value of a time series is dependent on one or more past observations in that series, plus noise)." (p. 1212)

"A key assumption underpinning our analysis is that the data are generated by underlying stochastic processes that are stationary. Therefore, it is important to investigate the robustness of the network structure over time. We adopted an ad hoc approach in which individual sDBNs are constructed for three splits of the available data." (p. 1212)

"We chose a monthly time step for our calculations given most of the variables considered are available as monthly series. Therefore, the lightning-ignition series was aggregated by month." (p. 1212)

"Application of more sophisticated conventional techniques (such as k-fold cross-validation) to time series can be problematic in three respects: (1) there is an implicit temporal dependence in such data (which might include seasonality and a chronic trend); (2) data leakage from the response to lagged variables is more likely to occur (i.e., information from the test set is inappropriately incorporated into the training set); and (3) the possible presence of non-stationarity in the series (due, for example, to temporal variations in the mean and variance)." (p. 1213)

"The lightning ignition data for the Warren region covers the period from April 1976 to December 2016. The monthly record consists of 489 observations, 95 of which are non-zero lightning-ignition counts. Observation platforms for fire detection and reporting protocols have remained stable over the period of record, and changes in land use are unlikely to have altered the pattern of lightning ignition." (pp. 1215 to 1216)

"Fifth, there are no direct interactions between LICt and the climate mode indices. There is the directed arc FDIt → LICt but no arc from FDIt-1 to LICt. This suggests that the lightning-ignition process is itself conditioned on concurrent weather and fuel moisture conditions." (p. 1217)

"The rolling origin evaluation is performed under the following settings: N = 489 (the number of months in the period April 1976 to December 2016); h = 12 months (covering July to June of the following year), fm = 0.7, and M = 351 ≥ ⌈fm ∗ N⌉ which ensures that the last observation in all training sets corresponds to the month of June. The size of the complete test data set is, of course, N − M = 138." (p. 1218)

"The equivalent rDBN and sDBN predictions of LICt exhibit a slight underestimation for the interval 0 ≤ LICt < 5, where 126 of the 138 observations in the test set lie (Fig. 6b). However, both DBNs underestimate the upper tail of the LICt distribution." (p. 1219)

"The strength probabilities for the arc DMIt-1 → N34t-1 were 0.50, 0.81 and 0.90 for the fm = 0.7, 0.85 and 1.0 cases, respectively. This result indicates a marginal to moderate increase in the strength of the relationship in the latter part of the study period. While this result may be attributable to noise, it is also consistent with recent evidence that the strength of the relationship is not time invariant (Lim et al. 2017)." (p. 1220)

"The ignition counts data in our case study are well described by a negative binomial distribution.
