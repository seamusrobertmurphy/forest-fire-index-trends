Question Q1, as a Canadian trend study of Fire Weather Index System components and the fire behaviour measures built on them, 1978 to 2023 from gridded ERA5 weather at local solar noon, by Theil-Sen slopes and the original Mann-Kendall test at the 10 per cent level with autocorrelation inspected but not corrected and no multiple-testing correction.

Establishes Benoît, Boulanger, Boucher and Girardin, Landscape Ecology 41, article 3, 23 pages, computed daily potential head fire intensity and rate of spread from the Canadian Forest Fire Behavior Prediction System for every 14 ha fuel cell of Quebec's 54.3 million hectare intensive protection zone, 1 April to 31 October of 1978 to 2023, from the ERA5-FWI-SN daily fire weather of the ESCER Centre, whose Fine Fuel Moisture Code and Buildup Index were computed from hourly ERA5 temperature, wind and humidity and 24 hour precipitation taken at local solar noon, and from yearly fuel maps of 1977 to 2022. Three runs separated the drivers, historical fuels with historical weather, the 1977 fuels held fixed under historical weather, and historical fuels under a fixed spread day weather of FFMC 91, BUI 42 and wind 12 km per hour. Yearly means and the count of days above 10,000 kW per metre were trended at the fuel cell, ERA5 cell and fire regime zone levels by the Theil-Sen estimator with the original Mann-Kendall test at p below 0.1, after inspecting the autocorrelation function, with no variance correction and no multiple-testing correction across the cells; fire weather components themselves, as seasonal means and 90th percentiles, were trended the same way at the ERA5 cell level. Under historical conditions the mean head fire intensity rose 12.9 kW per metre per decade with 46 per cent of cells significant, 27.5 per cent of them positive, and the count of days above 10,000 kW per metre rose 0.4 days over the period at the study area level, most in the western and northern zones; with fuels held at 1977 the slope rose to 15.6 kW per metre per decade, about 21 per cent more, while with weather held fixed over 70 per cent of cells showed no trend and the northern tier of zones fell. No fire occurrence was modelled, no station data were used, hourly against once-daily input was not compared, and no index version was named beyond Van Wagner (1987).
