Question Q3, how daily fire occurrence has been modelled against fire danger in Canada and whether the danger to occurrence relationship was tested for change over time.

Establishes Granville, Woolford, Dean and McFayden modelled the daily odds of an industrial forestry fire in Ontario from 1976 to 2019 against potential head fire intensity by conditional logistic regression on 775 matched sets, and tested whether the danger to ignition relationship differed between three regulatory periods with interaction terms and a likelihood ratio test. The slope over low intensity did not differ between periods, and the only difference over high intensity was between the earliest period and the current one. Journal of Agricultural, Biological, and Environmental Statistics 28(2), 219 to 242.

"Rather than modelling counts, we investigate the odds of igniting an IDF fire with the aim of determining MIOP's impact by contrasting it against Woods Guidelines and the time period preceding the introduction of Woods Guidelines (henceforth referred to as Pre-Woods)." (p. 220)

"We analyzed data for the period 1976–2019, describing a total of 61,862 wildland fires, of which 1175 were industrial forestry-caused (IDF) fires." (p. 222)

That is, it was OK to consider a single term for low HFI to represent this linear relationship for all three considered time periods." (pp. 232 to 233)
