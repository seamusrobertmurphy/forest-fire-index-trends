Subject. The chapter concerned the physics behind the fuel moisture codes, the equilibrium moisture content, sorption and its hysteresis, moisture diffusivity, and the response time and time lag of dead fine fuels.

Establishes. Nelson reviewed the water relations of dead and live forest fuels from the literature up to 2000. For dead fine fuels the chapter defined response time as the time for 1 minus 1/e, or 63.2 per cent, of the moisture change after a step change in conditions, and reserved the word timelag, following Viney and Catchpole (1991), for the lag of fuel moisture behind the diurnally varying equilibrium value. Classical diffusion theory gave response time proportional to the square of particle thickness under internal control and to thickness under surface control (equations 40 and 42, PDF pp. 50 and 128 printed, whose symbols the text layer garbled). Dewaxed red pine needles and aspen leaves responded 5.5 and 2.5 times faster than untreated ones. Equilibrium moisture fell by about 0.024 per 10 degrees Celsius in pine and aspen litter. Wind set the drying rate of jack pine litter only in the first hour. The sinusoidal field method of Viney applied to fuels with response times of 3 hours or less. The chapter credited the Canadian diurnal fine fuel model to Van Wagner (1987) and the 0.5 mm canopy interception to the same source. It gave no measured response time in hours for the standard pine litter the Fine Fuel Moisture Code represents, and no number for the code's own time lag. Section III.C on live fuel moisture, PDF pp. 23 to 37, was not read, as the brief allowed.

Bears on

- Introduction, "Fire danger has been rated throughout Canada since 1970" (qmd L2188), the sentence "The time lag is the time the litter takes to lose most of the difference between its moisture and the value the air would bring it to." Nelson gave the exact figure, 63.2 per cent or 1 minus 1/e of the change after a step in conditions, so the gloss "most of the difference" is supported and could carry the number. Nelson also reserved "timelag" for the phase lag of fuel moisture behind the diurnal equilibrium and used "response time" for the 63 per cent figure, so the 2025 report's phrase "time lag function" is Byram's usage, which Nelson called applicable only to step changes.
- Introduction, "Fire danger has been rated throughout Canada since 1970" (qmd L2188), the clause on the time lag function "cut in half". The chapter reported no measured response time in hours for pine litter and no value for the code's time lag, so it cannot confirm or refute the halving. It adds the empirical context that wind fixed the jack pine litter drying rate in the first hour, that dewaxed needles responded 5.5 times faster, and that the sinusoidal field method held for response times of 3 hours or less.
- Fire Danger Systems, "FWI2025 [@cffdg2025fwi2025] kept those components and computed them hourly." (qmd L2465), the clause "followed the hourly method of @vanwagner1977hourly". Nelson credited the Canadian diurnal fine fuel model to Van Wagner (1987), not 1977, and described it as exponential wetting and drying by vapour exchange with rain wetting set by rainfall amount and yesterday's moisture. The chapter does not contradict the 1977 attribution, since it did not cite that report, but it is not a source for it.
- Fire Danger Systems, "FWI2025 [@cffdg2025fwi2025] kept those components and computed them hourly." (qmd L2465), the sentence "Canopy interception of the first 0.5 mm of rain became cumulative within a rain event". Nelson stated the FWI1987 rule as net rainfall less a 0.5 mm correction for canopy interception, which supports the baseline the sentence changes from.
- Introduction, "The report's section 4.1 nonetheless claimed continuity" (qmd L2200). Nelson stated that equilibrium moisture fell by about 0.024 per 10 degrees Celsius in pine and aspen litter and that fine fuels rarely reach equilibrium in the field. A faster response therefore moves the code towards the equilibrium value more often, which is the mechanism behind the manuscript's expectation that absolute threshold counts move while the value of 91 holds.
- Index Computation, "The coefficient that converted the Fine Fuel Moisture Code to a moisture content" (qmd L2565). Nelson gave the fibre saturation and sorption background for moisture contents expressed as fractions, but gave no constant for the code scale, so the chapter neither supports nor contradicts the 147.2 against 147.2772 point.
