Question Q3, how daily fire activity has been modelled against daily gridded fire weather across Canada and which components performed best.

Establishes Wang, Oliver, Swystun, Hanes, Erni and Flannigan matched a daily fire growth database to daily gridded fire weather for 2001 to 2019, identified spread day indicators with a generalised additive model and predicted spread days with a semi binomial regression by ecozone and season, and found the Fine Fuel Moisture Code, Initial Spread Index and Fire Weather Index performed best, with the Fine Fuel Moisture Code most consistent. The paper is open access but Elsevier blocked retrieval on 2026-10-13 and the NRCan repository record holds no file, so only the OpenAlex abstract was read and the thresholds and validation could not be quoted. Science of the Total Environment 869, 161831.

Quotations

"This study explores the relationships between fire weather and spread days in the forested areas of Canada by spatially and temporally matching a daily fire growth database to a daily gridded fire weather database that spans from 2001 to 2019." (abstract)

"Using two modeling approaches, we were able to identify spread day indicators (generalized additive model) and to predict the occurrence of spread days (semi-binomial regression model) by Canadian Ecozones both annually and seasonally." (abstract)

"Overall, Fine Fuel Moisture Code (FFMC), Initial Spread Index (ISI), and Fire Weather Index (FWI) performed the best in all models built for spread day identification and prediction but varied depending on the conditions mentioned above. FFMC was the most consistent across all spatial and temporal scales." (abstract)
