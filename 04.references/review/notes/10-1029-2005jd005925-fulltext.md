Subject. The paper tested hourly relative humidity at 75 Canadian airport stations over 1953 to 2003 for steps from changes of instrument and procedure, chiefly the dewcel replacing the psychrometer.

Establishes. Over 1953 to 2003, the authors examined hourly relative humidity at 75 airport stations across Canada. The records came from the National Climate Data and Information Archive of the Meteorological Service of Canada. A station entered if it had run at least 40 years of the period with under 1 per cent of values missing. Stations near Toronto, Vancouver and Montreal were left out because of possible urban heat islands. The archive's stations were run by several organisations, the Canadian Defence Department, the Meteorological Service of Canada and volunteer observers among them. The paper did not say which organisation ran each of the 75. From the early 1970s, the dewcel, a sensor reading the conductivity of lithium chloride, replaced the wet and dry bulb psychrometer at most airport stations. Installation dates were found for 58 stations, 14 had maintenance records without a date, and 3 never used a dewcel. Table 1 named those three as Greenwood A, Shearwater A and Bagotville A. Table 1, on printed page 5 of 9, dated the installations from 22 August 1968 at Mont Joli A to 8 August 1993 at Peace River A. A count of its 58 dated rows gave 16 in 1970, 16 in 1971 and 7 in 1972. The text said instead that most were installed in 1971 and 1972, which matched Figure 3 as plotted. Measured by pixel, the counts of Figure 3 from 1973 onward equalled those of Table 1 from 1972 onward, so the figure appeared to plot dates one year late. Its counts for 1969 to 1972 were 2, 5, 17 and 12, against 1, 3, 16 and 16 in Table 1 for 1968 to 1971. At the 58 stations with an installation date, each seasonal series was fitted with a linear trend and again with a step added at that date. The step model was kept when its F statistic exceeded the 95th percentile of the F distribution with 1 and n − 3 degrees of freedom, here 4.07. Figure 5, on printed page 7 of 9, showed the step at 45 stations in winter, 28 in spring, 16 in summer and 31 in fall. Every step was a decrease except one increase, at Estevan in summer. As printed on page 6 of 9, winter steps ran from −3.5 to −18.3 per cent relative humidity and spring steps from −3.4 to −11.4. The text layer dropped these minus signs, and the paper gave no range for summer or fall. The steps were most common at cold stations. Every Arctic station north of 60°N had one in winter and spring, and southern and coastal stations were less affected. The authors judged the step probably due to high psychrometer readings when ice coated a bulb. They named the wet bulb on printed pages 1 and 3 and the dry bulb on page 8. The change to finer psychrometric tables in the 1960s was judged to have no major effect on trends. The 1977 metric conversion of the digital archive left the 1 per cent resolution of humidity unchanged. The dewcel was the only instrument change the paper tested. Before the steps were allowed for, 42 stations in winter and 46 in spring had significant decreasing trends, ranging from −2.2 to −17.4 per cent over 50 years. Summer and fall had 24 and 26, and these trends were much weaker. Afterwards, 10 winter and 23 spring trends remained significant. The spring ones ran from −2.4 to −12.3 per cent over 50 years, many of them in western Canada. The authors cautioned that part of each step could be climatic, and that precise adjustment would need a laboratory comparison of the two instruments, especially below −25 °C. Table 1 listed 11 British Columbia stations, Abbotsford, Comox, Fort Nelson, Fort St John, Port Hardy, Prince George, Prince Rupert, Quesnel, Sandspit, Smithers and Victoria. Their dewcels went in between 1970 and 1983, with the Victoria date uncertain. None lay in the Okanagan basin, and the paper did not name which of them had a step. Fire weather stations, forest agencies and the BC Wildfire Service appeared nowhere in the text.

Manuscript sentences checked

1. qmd L3619, "In the national network, @vanwijngaarden2005examination found that replacing the psychrometer by the dewcel "produced a decreasing step in relative humidity at a number of stations" [@vanwijngaarden2005examination, abstract].". Verdict, holds but the citation should give a page. Evidence, the abstract sat on PDF page 1, printed page 1 of 9 of article D22102, and read "It was found that the replacement of the psychrometer by the dewcel has produced a decreasing step in relative humidity at a number of stations." (PDF p. 1, printed p. 1 of 9). The quoted words were an exact run of that sentence. The paper never used the word network. Its stations were 75 airport stations in the archive of the Meteorological Service of Canada, run by several organisations. The sentence left out what a reader needs to judge the risk to this record. The step was most common in winter and at cold stations. Of the 58 stations with a known installation date, 45 had a significant step in winter and 16 in summer, by Figure 5 on printed page 7 of 9. The abstract stated "It seems that the southern and coastal stations are not as much affected by this change of instruments." (PDF p. 1, printed p. 1 of 9). The replacement began "in the early 1970s at most airport stations" (PDF p. 2, printed p. 2 of 9), about two decades before this record began in 1991. The paper covered no BC Wildfire Service station, and none of its 11 British Columbia stations lay in the Okanagan basin. Without these facts, a reader could take the step as a likely bias in May to September humidity at the study's stations, which the source did not show. The least change is to replace "abstract" with "p. 1". The fuller proposed wording follows.

   At 75 airport stations across Canada over 1953 to 2003, @vanwijngaarden2005examination found that replacing the psychrometer by the dewcel "produced a decreasing step in relative humidity at a number of stations" [@vanwijngaarden2005examination, p. 1]. Of the 58 stations with a known installation date, 45 had a significant step in winter and 16 in summer [@vanwijngaarden2005examination, pp. 6–7]. Southern and coastal stations were judged "not as much affected by this change of instruments" [@vanwijngaarden2005examination, p. 1].
