Question. Q2.

Establishes. van Wijngaarden and Vincent (Journal of Geophysical Research 110, D22102, 2005) examined hourly relative humidity at 75 Canadian stations over 1953 to 2003, found that replacing the psychrometer with the dewcel produced a decreasing step at a number of stations, more in winter than summer and less at southern and coastal stations, that the step accentuated the decreasing winter and spring trends, and that after the step was accounted for spring relative humidity in western Canada still fell by several per cent. Tsinko (2016, p. 14) reports from this paper that some winter differences reached 10 to 15 per cent, a figure that could not be checked against the paper itself.
