Question. Q1, as the 1991 to 2020 reference period and 95th percentile convention.

Establishes. Jain and 23 co-authors (Nature Communications 15, 6764, 2024) described the 2023 season, about 15 million hectares burned, against a 1991 to 2020 baseline, defining extreme fire weather at each ERA5 grid cell as Fire Weather Index above its 1991 to 2020 fire season 95th percentile, and found the mean May to October temperature 2.2 degrees Celsius above that baseline, the 2023 proportion of forest above the 95th percentile the highest since at least 1940, and embers crossing Okanagan Lake toward Kelowna. No trend test is reported; the paper is a reference for the baseline and the percentile convention.

Quotations

"Anthropogenic climate change enabled sustained extreme fire weather conditions, as the mean May–October temperature over Canada in 2023 was 2.2 °C warmer than the 1991–2020 average." (p. 1)

"We calculated a climatology of extreme FWI values at each ERA5 grid cell, defined as the 95th percentile of FWI values (FWI95) between 1991–2020 (omitting values outside of the fire season)." (p. 11)

"Embers transported over 3 km across Okanagan Lake started spot fires that threatened the city of Kelowna." (p. 2)
