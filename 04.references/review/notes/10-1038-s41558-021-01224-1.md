Question Q1, a gridded FWI trend covering Canada with Theil-Sen slopes, the Mann-Kendall test and a false discovery rate correction across grid cells but no stated serial autocorrelation treatment, and Q2 at the margin, because the daily input was taken from hourly ERA5 at local noon.

Establishes Jain, Castellanos-Acuna, Coogan, Abatzoglou and Flannigan, in the preprint of the article published in Nature Climate Change 12 (the saved text carries no journal pagination), computed the FWI and ISI from hourly ERA5 fields sampled at local noon on the 31 km grid for 1979 to 2020, with 24 hour precipitation summed from hourly totals, a start and stop rule on three consecutive days of maximum temperature below 5 or above 12 degrees Celsius after Wotton and Flannigan (1993), and an overwintered Drought Code after McElhinny and others (2020). Each year's 95th percentile of FWI, ISI and vapour pressure deficit was taken within a fixed fire season set per biome from the Global Fire Atlas for 2003 to 2016, and trends over the 42 years were estimated by the Theil-Sen slope and tested by the Mann-Kendall test with the false discovery rate controlled after Wilks (2006, 2016) at a global level of 0.05, with no correction for serial autocorrelation stated. Significant rises in FWI95 covered 26.0 per cent of the global burnable land, 12.5 per cent of North America, and the mean of significant FWI95 trends was a rise from 34.4 to 46.7, 35.8 per cent, over 41 years; boreal biomes rose less and less often than tropical and temperate ones. A partial Mann-Kendall test attributed significant FWI95 trends to relative humidity in 75.0 per cent of cells and to temperature in 40.4 per cent, against 11.3 per cent for precipitation and 10.6 per cent for wind. The study used no stations, no fire occurrence model and no comparison of index versions, and the authors noted that the fixed fire seasons may themselves be changing.

"We present a global analysis of trends in extreme fire weather from 1979–2020." (p. 2)

"Significant increases in extreme (95th percentile) annual values of the Fire Weather Index (FWI95), Initial Spread Index (ISI95), and Vapour Pressure Deficit (VPD95) occurred over 26.0%, 26.1%, and 46.1% of the global burnable landmass, respectively." (p. 2)

"Relative humidity and temperature were identified as the drivers of significant trends in FWI95 and ISI95 in most regions, largely where temperature trends outpaced dew point trends." (p. 2)

"Altogether, for the entire global burnable area the mean value over the 41-year period for FWI95 increased by 13.3% (i.e. from 29.8 to 33.8), while ISI95 increased by 11.5% (from 12.6 to 14.0), and VPD95 increased by 12.3% (from 26.6 to 29.9)." (p. 5)

"There were also increasing trends in extreme fire weather in boreal ecosystems, which have experienced a relatively high proportion (26.8%) of extreme wildfire events worldwide (Bowman et al. 2017), although the significance and size of these trends were generally smaller compared with tropical, subtropical, and temperate biomes (Tables S2, S3 and S4)." (p. 6)
