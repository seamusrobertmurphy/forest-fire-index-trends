Question Q2, how the weather input of the FWI is assembled at a nominal local noon from hourly forecast strips and how a change of driving reanalysis shifts the index level, with biases largest in mountains, and Q1 at the margin as the gridded FWI record that later trend studies over Canada draw on.

Establishes Vitolo, Di Giuseppe, Barnard, Coughlan, San-Miguel-Ayanz, Liberta and Krzeminski, Scientific Data 7, 216, pages 1 to 11, described GEFF-ERA5, the ECMWF reanalysis of the seven Canadian FWI System components computed with the GEFF model version 3.1 from ERA5 temperature, humidity, precipitation and wind for 1979 to 2019, at 0.25 degrees for the deterministic run and 0.5 degrees for a ten-member ensemble, with the first year discarded as spin-up. The input fields were cut into one-hour strips from the first 24 hours of each forecast and joined so that every grid cell was represented at about 12:00 local noon, no start-up or overwintering rule was applied, and the series was issued at a daily step. Validation was against two other reanalyses rather than observations, because the authors held the FWI not to be observable; against GEFF-ERAI and the GEOS-5 based Global Fire Weather Database the regional mean bias ran from -5 to +5 and the anomaly correlation from 0.52 to 0.87, with Boreal North America showing a bias of 5.34 against GFWED and 2.69 against GEFF-ERAI, and the largest biases fell in mountainous areas where the finer ERA5 orography changed precipitation placement. An illustration at Pedrogao Grande decomposed June FWI into linear trend, seasonal and random parts and found a rise from 13 to 17 over 40 years that the ensemble spread of 2 to 10 encompassed, which the authors read as not significant. The paper reported no station comparison, no formal trend estimator and no comparison of index versions.

"Thus atmospheric fields (e.g. temperature) are cut into 1-hourly time strips using the closest forecast step and then concatenated together so that the final field is representative of the conditions around the local noon within the 1-h resolution available." (p. 2)

"Data are made available in NetCDF format with a daily time step at 12 noon local time on a regular unprojected grid with spherical coordinates expressed in decimal degrees (EPSG:4326)." (p. 2)
