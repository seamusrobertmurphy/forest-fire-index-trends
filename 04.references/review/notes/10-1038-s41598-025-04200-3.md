Question Q2, because it shows that the weather input, station observations against ERA5 reanalysis at the same hour, changes the level, the tail and the trend of the Fire Weather Index, with the tail cut by about 25 per cent and the trend by 23 to 82 per cent; and Q1 as a station trend comparator, since it is one of the few seventy-year trend series of observed station fire danger.

Establishes Sjöström, Vermina Plathner and Granström, Scientific Reports 15, article 20111, pages 1 to 15, computed the full Fire Weather Index System from noon (12.00 UTC) observations of temperature, relative humidity and 10 m wind and 18.00 UTC 24 hour precipitation at nine Swedish stations across 1100 km of latitude for 1951 to 2020, with Van Wagner and Pickett (1985) equations and no Drought Code overwintering, and summarised each season by the peak seven-day moving average of the index, FWI-7x. Trends were fitted by ordinary linear regression over the 70 years, with no Theil-Sen or Mann-Kendall estimator, no autocorrelation treatment and no multiple-testing correction across the nine stations and the many codes; the authors said the Mann-Kendall test was of limited value with nine points. FWI-7x rose at eight of nine stations, significantly (p < 0.05) at Storuman, Falun and Västervik, by 2.9 index points on average, 18 per cent of the period mean (confidence interval 0 to 37 per cent), with temperature the main cause through a lower relative humidity. Against ERA5 values for the same cells and hours, the reanalysis index was about 75 per cent of the observed index for moderate and high fire danger, 5 points lower at FWI 20 and 8 points at FWI 30, and the reanalysis trends were 23 to 82 per cent smaller, with the sign reversed at Jokkmokk. The paper computed no fire occurrence model and did not compare hourly against daily input or index versions.

On average, annual peak 7-day moving-average FWI increased by 18% over 70 years." (p. 1)
