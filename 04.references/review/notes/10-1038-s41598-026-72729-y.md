Establishes
Campos, Couto, Guiomar, Picos and Salgado computed an hourly FWI on a 500 m Meso-NH grid for three fire episodes in Portugal and Spain, 2021 to 2025, initialised from the daily Copernicus ERA5 product, and found a diurnal cycle peaking at 1500 to 1600 UTC and a bias of the hourly model value at 1200 UTC against the daily product of +3.02, +4.53 and −5.71 FWI units in the three cases, so hourly input shifts the level and the within-day tail but no trend was examined. The saved PDF (25 pages, 11,502 words, article in press so page numbers are PDF pages) was searched and holds no mention of FWI2025, the 2025 update, NG-CFFDRS or CFFDRS2025; it computes the hourly FFMC with Van Wagner's 1987 equations.

Quotations
"FWI is typically computed once per day using weather observations at standard local noon, representing assumed peak fire activity during the afternoon." (p. 2)

"Furthermore, as it is a daily product calculated once a day (1200 UTC), it does not capture the sub-daily dynamics of meteorological variables." (p. 7)

"The temporal evolution of FWI and FFMC in P1 and P2 shows a diurnal cycle: minimum values occur during the night, then increase progressively after sunrise, reaching a maximum between 1500 UTC and 1600 UTC, subsequently decreasing toward early morning." (p. 9)

"The cases of Serra da Estrela and Castro Marim show a positive BIAS of 3.02 and 4.53, respectively, indicating that Meso-NH is overestimating the FWI; on the other hand, the BIAS value for Galicia was − 5.71, indicating an underestimation by the model." (p. 17)

"It is important to note that conventional FWI’s daily forecasts calculated at local noon remain essential for resource allocation and public alert levels, however hourly FWI forecasts provide dynamic situational awareness throughout the day." (p. 21)
