Question Q1 at the margin, as a gridded trend in days above a fire danger percentile that covers western North America and supplies a block bootstrap treatment of serial autocorrelation for the Mann-Kendall and Pettitt tests with a false discovery rate correction, and Q2 at the margin, because the percentile threshold is fixed from the whole record rather than a reference period; the index is the McArthur FFDI from reanalysis, not the Canadian FWI.

Establishes Richardson, Black, Irving, Matear, Monselesan, Risbey, Squire and Tozer, npj Climate and Atmospheric Science 5, 23, pages 1 to 12, computed the McArthur Forest Fire Danger Index from the JRA55 reanalysis at 1.25 degrees for 1958 to 2020, using daily maximum temperature, daily mean relative humidity, daily maximum wind and a 20 day rolling precipitation drought factor, and counted fire weather days as days above the grid cell's 95th percentile taken over all data, accumulated over a 12 month fire year centred on the month of peak FFDI. The Canadian FWI was set aside because its start-up and snow rules were held to be more demanding. Trends in annual fire weather days for 1970 to 2020 were Theil-Sen slopes tested by the Mann-Kendall test, with step changes by the Pettitt test, both given p values from 1000 block bootstrap series whose block length, between 1 and 11 years, was set per grid cell from the lag-one autocorrelation under a first-order autoregressive assumption, and the false discovery rate was controlled at 0.1 across grid cells. Fire weather days rose significantly over western North America, Brazil, eastern Europe and western Asia and south-east Australia, with step changes in the 1990s to early 2000s, while antecedent precipitation showed few significant trends, so that the rise in compound drought and fire weather years was attributed to fire weather rather than rainfall; in western North America there were eight fire weather years since 1990, two of them coinciding with drought, and burned area for 2001 to 2019 rose with fire weather days over most of the globe. The paper reported no station series, no Canadian index and no fire occurrence model.
