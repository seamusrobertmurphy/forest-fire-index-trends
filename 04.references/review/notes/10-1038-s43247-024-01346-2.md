Question Q1, because it gives the 2021 British Columbia fire weather record from ERA5 and the Theil-Sen slope as its detrending estimator, and Q2, because it shows the Fire Weather Index breaking few records in 2021 while temperature and vapour pressure deficit broke many, which it traced to near normal wind, and because it computed a nighttime hourly Fine Fuel Moisture Code with the Van Wagner (1977) method on hourly ERA5 input alongside the daily index.

Establishes Jain, Sharma, Castellanos Acuna, Abatzoglou and Flannigan, Communications Earth and Environment 5, article 202, pages 1 to 10, tracked the late June 2021 Pacific Northwest heat dome as a persistent positive anomaly in ERA5 500 hPa geopotential height over 35 to 75 degrees north and 60 to 149 degrees west, finding it lasted 27 days from 18 June to 14 July 2021, reached 11.6 million square kilometres and a 241 m anomaly, and was the strongest of 450 June to August events from 1979 to 2021, 36 per cent stronger than the next in 2003. Fire weather from hourly ERA5 at 0.25 degrees, 1979 to 2021, with the Drought Code overwintered after McElhinny et al. (2020) and the indices computed in the cffdrs R package, showed daily maximum temperature records broken in 14 per cent of grid cells over Canada and the United States in 2021 and vapour pressure deficit records in 11.5 per cent, but Fire Weather Index records in only 2.67 per cent, 14 per cent of them inside the heat dome, which the authors attributed to near normal wind speed; mean nighttime hourly Fine Fuel Moisture Code records fell in 2.9 per cent of cells. Removing the 1979 to 2020 Theil-Sen linear trend in geopotential height left an event 59 per cent shorter, 34 per cent smaller and 6 per cent lower in amplitude, so the observed event was 86 per cent stronger than it would have been without warming. By MODIS MCD64A1, British Columbia burned 0.7 Mha, 0.48 per cent of its land area, in 2021, and 20.6 per cent of North American burned area started inside the heat dome, rising to 34 per cent within ten days after it. Synchronous fire danger for Canada was days with the Fire Weather Index above its cell wise 95th percentile over more than 10 per cent of the forested full suppression zone, which correlated 0.80 with days at national preparedness level 4 or higher. The paper gave no trend estimate for the fire danger indices, no Mann-Kendall test, no occurrence count model, and no comparison of hourly against daily Fire Weather Index values.
