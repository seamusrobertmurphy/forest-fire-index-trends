Question Q3, how daily fire occurrence has been modelled against fire weather in British Columbia, with what validation, and whether the relationship varied between years.

Establishes Magnussen and Taylor modelled daily fire presence in 950 twenty kilometre cells of British Columbia from 1981 to 2000 by binary logistic regression on lightning, forest cover, weather, stability indices and the fuel moisture codes, refitted the coefficients for each year because their influence varied from year to year, averaged them for prediction, and validated by leave one year out, with R of 0.83 for lightning and 0.55 for human caused daily counts at the provincial level. No open copy exists and the CSIRO hosts blocked retrieval on 2026-10-13, so only the Crossref abstract was read, as in the manuscript already; the year to year variation is the one sentence the abstract adds on change over time. International Journal of Wildland Fire 21(4), 342 to 356.

Quotations

"Daily records of the location and timing of human- and lightning-caused fires in British Columbia from 1981 to 2000 were used to estimate the probability of fire occurrence within 950 20 × 20-km spatial units (~950 000 km2) using a binary logistic regression modelling framework." (abstract)

"Because the influence of the explanatory variables in the models varied from year to year, model coefficients were estimated for each year. The arithmetic mean of the model coefficients was used for making daily predictions in a future year." (abstract)

"A leave-1-year-out cross-validation procedure was used to assess model performance for random years. The daily number of lightning-caused fires was reasonably well predicted at the provincial level (R = 0.83) and slightly less well predicted for a smaller (75 000 km2) administrative region." (abstract)

"The daily number of human-caused fires was less well predicted at both the provincial (R = 0.55) and the regional level." (abstract)
