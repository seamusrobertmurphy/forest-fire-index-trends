Question. Q2.

Establishes. Chavardès, Daniels, Eskelson and Pickell (International Journal of Wildland Fire 28, 445 to 456, 2019) compared three monthly adaptations of the Drought Code with the monthly mean of daily values at the Palliser fire weather station in south eastern British Columbia, two of which add overwinter drying, an early season start and better effective precipitation, and used them against a fire scar record of 17 fire years from 1901 to 2013. The startup and overwinter handling changes the monthly code enough to add significant April and May fire drought associations; no trend is reported.

Quotations

"The three adaptations were compared with the monthly mean DC calculated from daily values for the Palliser fire-weather station." (Abstract)

"Two adaptations improved on the existing Monthly DC calculated from monthly climate data by (1) accounting for overwinter drying and an early start to the fire season, and (2) improving estimates of effective precipitation." (Abstract)

"Of the 17 fire years from 1901 to 2013, 6 years had low initial drought conditions that increased late in the fire season, and 5 years had high drought conditions throughout the fire season." (Abstract)
