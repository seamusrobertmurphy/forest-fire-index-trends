Question Q3, how daily fire occurrence has been modelled against fire danger across British Columbia and with what validation.

Establishes Nadeem, Taylor, Woolford and Dean modelled daily human and lightning fire presence by grid cell across British Columbia with lasso logistic regression on an ensemble of 500 balanced datasets and a baseline risk covariate, and reported areas under the receiver operating characteristic curve above 0.9. The paper is open access, but the CSIRO site moved to connectsci.au and both hosts blocked retrieval on 2026-10-13, so only the Crossref abstract was read; the study period, the seasonal terms and the hold out design could not be quoted. International Journal of Wildland Fire 29(1), 11 to 27.

Quotations

"We developed three models of daily human- and lightning-caused fire occurrence to support fire management preparedness and detection planning in the province of British Columbia, Canada, using a lasso-logistic framework." (abstract)

"Novel aspects of our work involve (1) using an ensemble of models that were created using 500 datasets balanced (through response-selective sampling) to have equal numbers of fire and non-fire observations; (2) the use of a new ranking algorithm to address the difficulty in interpreting variable importance in models with a large number of covariates." (abstract)

"All three models have strong predictive ability, with areas under the Receiver Operator Characteristic curve exceeding 0.9." (abstract)
