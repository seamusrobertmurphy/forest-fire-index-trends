Establishes
Plucinski, Sullivan and McCaw compared daily maximum FFDI with hourly FFDI summary metrics against five fire activity statistics in six forested areas of southern Australia and found the maximum hourly FFDI best but no metric significantly different from the others, so the choice of daily summary moved fire activity skill little. Only the abstract was readable; the publisher page at connectsci.au shows the full text behind a pay wall and no open copy was found, so no trend result and no number beyond the abstract are available.

Quotations
"Variations in daily maximum FFDI and the hourly changing of FFDI values during the day (including use of different soil moisture deficit indices) were compared against five binary fire activity statistics in six forested areas in southern Australia, with performance assessed using Theil–Sen regression lines fitted to rank percentile curves." (p. 926, abstract)

"Fire activity rates were similar on days with wide and narrow hourly FFDI distributions except in one study area where days with wide distributions experienced more fires." (p. 926, abstract)

"The maximum hourly FFDI metric performed the best of all the metrics tested, though there were no statistically significant differences among any of them." (p. 926, abstract)
