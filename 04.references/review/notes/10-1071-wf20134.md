Question Q3, what validation a daily fire occurrence model should carry.

Establishes Phelps and Woolford set out guidelines for evaluating daily fire occurrence models, the choice of a testing dataset, the metrics, temporal and spatial visualisation, the variability of metrics and collaboration with end users, with a Lac La Biche case from 1996 to 2016, and found many published machine learning models unsuited to operations. Open access at CSIRO, but both hosts blocked retrieval on 2026-10-13, so only the Crossref abstract was read and the specific recommendations on temporal hold out and proper scoring rules could not be quoted. International Journal of Wildland Fire 30(4), 225 to 240.

Quotations

"We demonstrate that the methodologies often used for evaluating and comparing FOP models may lead to selecting a model that is ineffective for operational use." (abstract)

"With an emphasis on spatially and temporally explicit FOP modelling for daily fire management operations, we outline and discuss several guidelines for evaluating and comparing data-driven FOP models, including choosing a testing dataset, choosing metrics for model evaluation, using temporal and spatial visualisations to assess model performance, recognising the variability in performance metrics, and collaborating with end users to ensure models meet their operational needs." (abstract)

"Our findings indicate that many machine learning FOP models in the historical literature are not well suited for fire management operations." (abstract)
