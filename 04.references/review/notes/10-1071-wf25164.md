Question Q3, whether fire occurrence timing has changed over time in Canada and at what rate.

Establishes Granville, Woolford and Dean defined each year's fire season start and end from observed fires by grid cell across Ontario and found season length rising at about 0.5 days per year in the northwest and 0.25 days per year in the northeast, with earlier human caused starts and later lightning caused ends. "A new algorithm that uses observed wildfires is developed to identify the timing of the start and end of each year's fire season. These are analyzed to quantify trends in the fire season over time and space." (abstract)

"Fire season length is increasing across much of northern Ontario, with earlier human-caused fire start dates and later lightning-caused fire end dates. Median rates of increase are about 0.5 days/year in the northwest and 0.25 days/year in the northeast." (abstract)

"Our findings corroborate past studies showing that Ontario's fire season is changing but provide more spatial insight as to where." (abstract)
