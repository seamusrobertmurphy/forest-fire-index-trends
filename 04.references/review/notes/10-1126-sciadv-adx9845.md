Subject. A global attribution of the 1980 to 2023 trend in days above the 95th percentile of the Fire Weather Index, from daily mean reanalysis and model weather.

Establishes. Turco and eight co-authors counted FWI95d, the yearly days with the Fire Weather Index above the 95th percentile of all daily values, for each grid cell. The definition followed Jones and others 2022, and the percentile was taken over every day of the year, with no fire season window. The baseline for the percentile was the whole analysis window, 1980 to 2023, for the reanalyses and forced simulations, and the past 449 years for control runs. The main text named no bootstrap or other correction for base-period bias, and the Supplementary Materials, which held more detail on the calculation, were not read. The text cited the index to Van Wagner 1987 and named no 2025 revision. It computed the index with the fireDanger R package v1.1.0, citing Iturbide and others 2019, Van Wagner and Pickett 1985 and Lawson and Armitage 2008. Its inputs were daily means of temperature, relative humidity and wind speed with 24 hour accumulated precipitation, not the local noon values the index was designed for. The choice rested on Matteo and others 2025, whose authors included eight of this text's nine. Data were the ERA5 and JRA55 reanalyses and 86 historical plus SSP2-4.5 simulations from 10 CMIP6 models, with preindustrial control runs for internal variability. The year 1979 was dropped as spin-up, and all inputs were remapped bilinearly to a 1 degree grid. Cells with more than 80 per cent bare areas, water, snow and ice or sparse vegetation were masked. Trends were fitted by ordinary least squares, and the Theil-Sen estimator gave negligible differences in pattern and magnitude. The signal to noise ratio of the fingerprint reached about 3 to 7 in the reanalyses, above a 99 per cent detection threshold of about 2.3. Detection held with the global mean removed, and without the regions where Matteo and others 2025 had found daily inputs failed to capture noon trends. The main text and captions printed no trend magnitude for any region, and no number for western North America or Canada. They named western North America a hotspot of pronounced positive trends, and the 2023 Canadian fire season only as consistent with escalating risk. The text did not test the index against fire records, and it measured no effect of input timing on a trend itself.

Bears on

- Discussion, "Of the trends cited here, the extreme days of @matteo2025challenges were percentile exceedances, which the version change left unchanged." (qmd L3576). Adds to it. The text described the metric of Matteo and others 2025 as "the yearly count of days above the local 95th percentile of daily FWI" (section 10, FWI calculation). Eight of its nine authors had also written that paper, so the account was not independent. Its own FWI95d was a percentile exceedance of the same kind, over a 1980 to 2023 baseline, from daily mean ERA5, JRA55 and CMIP6 weather. It computed one version of the System, so it could join the percentile trends cited but did not test the version effect.
- Discussion, "A percentile trend was portable across the version change only when its reference period was stated, kept fixed and corrected for the base-period bias." (qmd L3576; the Conclusions repeat the condition at L3664). Qualifies it. The text stated that its baseline "covers 1980–2023 for both observations and HIST+245 simulations and the past 449 years for the control runs" (section 10, FWI calculation). That was the whole analysis window, so no year lay outside the baseline, and the main text named no correction for base-period bias. As worded, the sentence would class this published trend as not portable. The sentence did not say whether a baseline spanning the whole record needed the correction.
- Discussion, "The one measured effect of input timing on a slope was the 0.22 to 0.39 days per decade by which daily aggregates inflated a global extreme-day trend [@matteo2025challenges]." (qmd L3568). Qualifies it. The text measured no effect of input timing on a slope. It cited Matteo and others 2025 as its reference 43, which "identified daily mean values as the least biased alternative for approximating FWI trends calculated with noon-specific data" (section 10, FWI calculation), and printed none of that paper's numbers. It also credited Herrera and others 2013 with a "deleterious effect on FWI trends" from daily mean inputs (section 10, FWI calculation). The repository's accepted manuscript of Herrera and others 2013 compared 2031 to 2050 with 1981 to 2000 by the delta method (PDF p. 6). It used the word trend only in its reference list (PDF p. 12). The claim held for fitted slopes, but a reader of this text could take Herrera's deltas as a second measured effect on trends.
- Introduction, "All of them predated the 2025 update, so every fire danger index among them was computed under FWI1987." (qmd L2190). Adds to it. Received in April 2025 and published in March 2026, the text still cited the index to Van Wagner 1987 and computed it "using the fireDanger R package [v1.1.0; (62–64)]" (section 10, FWI calculation). Its references 63 and 64 were Van Wagner and Pickett 1985 and Lawson and Armitage 2008, and it named no 2025 revision. Its inputs were daily means, while FWI2025 calculates from hourly weather. A trend study published after the update was therefore still a statement about FWI1987.
