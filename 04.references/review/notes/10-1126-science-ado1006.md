Establishes. Wang, Wang, Flannigan, Guindon, Swystun, Castellanos-Acuna, Wu and Wang (Science 387, 91 to 97, 2025) modelled burn severity across Canadian forests from 40 years of fire and weather data with multinomial logistic regression, found fuel aridity the strongest driver, and reported significant increases in the number of days conducive to high severity burning over about 6 per cent of the modelled area for 1981 to 2020, most of it in 2001 to 2020 and in spring and autumn. The estimator, weather source and corrections are in the paper and supplement, which could not be obtained, so the abstract alone is recorded.

Quotations

"About 6% (0.54 to 14.64%) of the modeled areas show significant increases in the number of days conducive to high-severity burning during 1981 to 2020, most of which were found during 2001 to 2020 and in the spring and autumn." (Abstract)

"Our results show that fuel aridity was the most influential driver of burn severity, summer months were more prone to severe burning, and the northern areas were most influenced by the changing climate." (Abstract)
