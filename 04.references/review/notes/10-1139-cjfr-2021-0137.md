Question. Q1, as an Okanagan input record and a projection, and Q2 through the station record used.

Establishes. Spittlehouse and Dymond (Canadian Journal of Forest Research 52, 2022; the University of Toronto TSpace accepted manuscript cjfr-2021-0137.R1 was read, so page numbers are manuscript pages) built 100 years of synthetic daily weather with LARS-WG5 from the Upper Penticton Creek P1 station (1984 to 2016) and Penticton Airport (1953 to 2018), computed the Fire Weather Index System with the cffdrs package along an elevation transect of the Penticton Creek watershed, found no significant trend in seasonal temperature or precipitation at either station over 1984 to 2016 by the zyp package, a historic fire season of 209, 135 and 107 days at low, moderate and high elevation, and projected increases of 14 to 32 days by the 2050s and up to 69 days by the 2080s. These are projections and an input check, not an observed Fire Weather Index trend.

Quotations

"There are no significant (p<0.2) increasing or decreasing trends in the seasonal precipitation or air temperature over the 1984 to 2016 period at Penticton Airport and P1 (calculated using the zyp R package Bronaugh and Werner 2013)." (manuscript p. 9)

"Environment and Climate Change Canada’s (ECCC) Penticton Airport station (49°27’47”N, 119°36’08”W, 344 m) ~21 km SW of P1 has daily and hourly values for these variables available from 1953 to 2018." (manuscript p. 9)

"The high elevation site had under half the increase in fire season length (14 versus 32 days) compared to the low site for the warming of the CNRM45 and MPI45 scenarios in the 2050s." (manuscript p. 17)

"The RCP4.5 scenario results do not change by 2080s while the RCP8.5 scenarios result in an increase fire season length by up to 69 days (51%) (Table S6)." (manuscript p. 17)

"For a landscape near to our study area, Nitschke and Innes (2008) project an increase in 17 days, mostly in the spring, by the 2050s." (manuscript p. 20)
