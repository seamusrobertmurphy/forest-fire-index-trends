Question. Q1, as the nearest Canadian precedent for a modified Mann Kendall test with Theil Sen slopes.

Establishes. Aftergood and Flannigan (Canadian Journal of Forest Research 52, 2022; the University of Toronto TSpace accepted manuscript cjfr-2021-0353.R3 was read, so page numbers are manuscript pages) tested lightning fire counts across western Canada for 1981 to 2018 with a modified Mann Kendall test under block bootstrap and bias corrected prewhitening after Hamed (2009) in the modifiedmk package, with Theil Sen slopes, and found a non significant decrease overall (tau minus 0.17, bootstrap p 0.05, Sen slope minus 20.44 fires per year) and in June, July and August, with British Columbia showing only isolated pockets of trend. No fire danger index is tested.

Quotations

"This process entailed using the nonparametric block bootstrapping and bias corrected prewhitening method and a modified Mann-Kendall test to account for type 1 errors and serial correlation in the data (Hamed, 2009) using the modifiedmk package in R Core Team (2021)." (manuscript p. 12)

"The Theil-Sen slope method was used to calculate the slope value (β)." (manuscript p. 12)

"The Mann Kendall statistic computed for the months of June (tau -0.15, p-value 0.105, z-score -1.29, sen slope 5.04), July (tau -0.039, p-value 0.36, z-score -0.32, sen slope -1.69), and August (tau -0.048, p-value 0.33, z-score -0.4, sen slope -3.5) all found non-significant decreases in the trend for NOF’s for the 38-year period." (manuscript p. 15)

"British Columbia experienced isolated pockets of decreasing trend for fire clusters in June, pockets of increasing trend for fire clusters in July and overall no trends detected outside of those pockets for all three months." (manuscript p. 18)

"The tau computed was -0.17, bootstrap p-value 0.05, z-score -1.5 and the sen slope -20.44." (manuscript p. 36, Figure 6 caption)
