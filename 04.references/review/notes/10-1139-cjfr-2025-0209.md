Question Q3, whether fire occurrence has changed over time in Canada and whether that change was related to fire danger.

Establishes Hanes, Jain, Wang, Wang, Parisien, Little and Flannigan updated the national trend analysis to 1959 to 2024 with Mann Kendall tests, Theil Sen slopes and segmented regression on annual counts and area burned, found human caused area burned declining to 2005 and rising since, and the number of large lightning fires in the Montane Cordillera rising at 0.50 fires per year, but fitted no fire weather and tested no danger to occurrence relationship, attributing the human caused rise to fire conducive weather by citation. The copy read is the accepted manuscript deposited in the University of Toronto TSpace, so page and line numbers are the manuscript's. Canadian Journal of Forest Research, research note.

Quotations

"In contrast to the preceding analysis, human-caused fires, which were previously detected as decreasing in annual area burned, have now been increasing with high confidence since the mid-2000's." (manuscript p. 2, lines 24 to 26)

"Seasonality was not included because a full analysis with fire weather was warranted (e.g. Jain et al., 2017) to fully understand the changes and was beyond the scope of this update." (manuscript p. 4, lines 99 to 100)

"As in our previous study, a nonparametric Mann–Kendall test was used to detect monotonic trends in time series data for all fire-regime attributes." (manuscript p. 5, lines 107 to 108)

(manuscript pp. 7 to 8, lines 193 to 196)
