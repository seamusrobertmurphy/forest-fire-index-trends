Establishes. Boulanger, Boucher, Nicoletta, Wang, Barber, Whitman and Parisien (Canadian Journal of Forest Research, 2026) used the Canadian Fire Spread Dataset for 2002 to 2024 and found the mean number of spread days per fire rose by 138 per cent, driven by more days of fire conducive weather, while mean daily spread distance fell by 43.9 per cent. The estimator and the fire weather threshold are in the full text, which could not be obtained.

Quotations

"We found that the mean number of days with observed fire spread per fire more than doubled (+138%) over the study period." (Abstract)

"This increase was driven by a growing number of days with fire-conducive weather, which promoted longer fire durations and greater cumulative potential spread." (Abstract)

"In contrast, mean daily spread distance (DSD) per fire declined substantially (−43.9%), while the frequency of extreme spread events (above the 90th percentile) remained relatively stable." (Abstract)
