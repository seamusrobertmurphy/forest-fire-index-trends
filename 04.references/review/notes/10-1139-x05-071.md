Subject. The text modelled, for each Ontario ecoregion, the probability that a lightning strike caused a fire later detected, and the daily probability of detecting a smouldering fire.

Establishes. In Ontario's fire management region, over the fire seasons of 1992 to 2001 with 1999 removed, the study fitted lightning fire models for 11 ecoregion units. Daily cloud-to-ground strikes from the provincial agency's own detection network were counted by polarity in 20 km cells for each day from 0000 to 2359 EDT. The source's map showed 15 sensors, and detection efficiency was put at 80 to 90 per cent in the intensively protected part, falling rapidly to the north. Noon weather and the FWI System codes from 50 to 150 stations were interpolated to cell centres by thin-plate spline. Each fire's ignition date was taken as the last day with lightning within 10 km before its detection. The ignition model was a logistic regression of fires per strike in each cell and day, fitted only to days with lightning. Expected fires were the sum of strike counts times the fitted probability per strike. Its terms were a sheltered duff moisture code (SDMC), the Drought Code, the Fine Fuel Moisture Code, positive strike fraction, closed conifer cover, storm timing, drying and rain. Neither the ignition model nor the detection model evaluated the Fire Weather Index, the Buildup Index or the Daily Severity Rating. The 11 ignition models held 79 to 908 fires and 3,829 to 38,293 observations each. The SDMC entered all 11 with coefficients of 0.018 to 0.060 and typically gave the largest share of model chi-square. The Drought Code entered all 11 with a sign that varied, and the Fine Fuel Moisture Code entered 8 (Table 1, printed pp. 1394–1395). Positive strike fraction entered 8 models with coefficients of 0.54 to 1.32. Overnight storms raised ignition probability in all 11 models, and rain over 5 mm in the 3 days after the strike lowered it in 10. No per-term chi-square was printed, and no model without strike counts was fitted. The ignition models had C statistics of 0.62 to 0.84. Annual ecoregion totals correlated with observations at 0.86 (p < 0.0001), explaining about 73 per cent of annual variability. For 2002 and 2003, obtained after fitting, the correlation was 0.66 (n = 21, p = 0.0007). Daily province totals after log transformation correlated at 0.63 (n = 560, p < 0.0001), and raw daily correlations by year ran from 0.70 to 0.93 (Table 4, printed p. 1399). Strikes per fire ran from 277 to 1,478 by ecoregion, and annual strikes in ecoregion 98 ran from 19,221 to 81,239 (Table 3, printed p. 1396). Holdover from ignition to detection decayed exponentially, and more than 75 per cent of fires were detected within 3 days of the strike. The daily detection models, on that day's indices, were only slight improvements on a constant probability, with C statistics of 0.61 to 0.79 (Table 2, printed pp. 1396–1397).

Bears on

- Introduction, "A danger rating system was judged on whether its outputs tracked fire occurrence." (qmd L2198). Adds to it, since models led by FWI System moisture codes, given strike counts, predicted Ontario's lightning fires over 1992 to 2001 with C statistics of 0.62 to 0.84 (printed pp. 1393, 1395).
- Methods, "Daily ignition counts were modelled by negative binomial regression" (qmd L2712). Qualifies it, because the source's lightning fires held over for days, and it warned that 24-hour days could split one overnight storm across 2 days (printed pp. 1391, 1393, 1398).
- Methods, "The linear predictor contained the series' Fire Weather Index averaged over the" (qmd L2712). Qualifies it, because the source's strongest lightning predictor was a sheltered duff moisture code, and neither of its models evaluated the Fire Weather Index (printed pp. 1389, 1391, 1393).
- Methods, "The lightning model had no strike term, because no public record of strikes covered the seasons." (qmd L2712). Qualifies it, because the source treated strikes as the number of trials rather than as a term, and left days without lightning out of the fit (printed pp. 1392, 1396).
- Methods, "The Canadian Lightning Detection Network" (qmd L2712). Qualifies it, since the source's strikes came from Ontario's own provincial network for 1992 onward, before the national network the sentence names (printed p. 1390).
- Discussion, "The small difference in ignition skill between series was consistent" (qmd L3572). Adds to it, because, even with strike counts, the source found "a great deal of unexplained variability" and called day-to-day comparison of small expected counts "problematic" (printed pp. 1395, 1397).
- Discussion, "Fixed classes were already regional and tied to the period that calibrated them." (qmd L3576). Adds to it, because the source called the FWI System's moisture codes "relative indicators of moisture levels", so similar duff code values in different forest types "may represent different absolute levels of moisture" (printed p. 1392).
