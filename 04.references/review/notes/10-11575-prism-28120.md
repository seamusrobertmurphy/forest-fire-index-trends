Question. Q2, with the estimator and the multiple testing correction also bearing on Q1.

Establishes. Tsinko (University of Calgary MSc thesis, 2016) recomputed Fine Fuel Moisture Code, Duff Moisture Code, Drought Code, Fire Weather Index and Daily Severity Rating at 16, 10 and 5 Environment Canada stations (27, 49 and 66 years of record, with Quesnel, Smithers, Terrace and Fort St John in British Columbia and none in the Okanagan) from raw and from homogenised temperature, precipitation and wind, and found that homogenisation changed the significance of the linear trend at up to half of the stations for the 90th percentile of a season's code values, with the Duff Moisture Code the most resistant and the wind dependent codes the least. The relative humidity breakpoints fell in the 1970s to 1990s at the dewcel changeover, the Drought Code was restarted each spring without overwintering because the years were not consecutive, and the Mann Kendall test with Sen slopes was run beside linear regression under a Benjamini and Hochberg false discovery rate field test.

"Trend significance of up to 50% of the Canadian weather stations considered was affected if computed using the homogenized data instead of the raw inhomogeneous records." (p. ii)

"Up to 50% of the stations experienced a change in linear trend significance in P90 of the code values, up to 40% in IQR and up to 20% in median values (Table 4.3)." (p. 48)
