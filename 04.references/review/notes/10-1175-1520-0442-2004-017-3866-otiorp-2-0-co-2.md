Question Q1 and Q2, because a fire danger trend computed from gridded reanalysis weather inherits any inhomogeneity in the reanalysis, and the paper shows how a change in the observing system feeding a product produces apparent change over time that is not climate, the same homogeneity problem that a change of index version or of weather input raises.

Establishes Sterl, in Journal of Climate 17(19), 3866 to 3873, compared the NCEP-NCAR reanalysis, which ran from 1948, with the ERA-40 reanalysis, which ran from September 1957 to August 2002, over the whole globe using monthly means and monthly anomalies with the climatology and a linear trend removed. The paper used two checks of homogeneity, the differences in sea level pressure and 500 hPa height between the two reanalyses along three meridional sections and the correlation between the monthly anomalies of latent heat flux and the rate of change of sea surface temperature, a relation known from Cayan (1992) to be strong in the extratropics. Differences between the reanalyses were systematic in sign throughout the record, which the author read as model differences, but their magnitude changed gradually in the Southern Hemisphere and suddenly in 1967, 1969, 1973, 1976, 1979 and 1993, the 1979 step coinciding with the arrival of satellite data. Correlations between the two reanalyses were low across much of the Southern Hemisphere in 1958 to 1967 and high nearly everywhere in 1989 to 1999, and the latent heat flux to sea surface temperature correlation, above 0.6 in the Northern Hemisphere in every period, was absent in the Southern Hemisphere until after 1981, when the sea surface temperature product changed to one with satellite retrievals. The paper concluded that Northern Hemisphere observation density constrained both models through the whole period, that Southern Hemisphere homogeneity could be assumed only after 1980, and that long-term variability in the early Southern Hemisphere record might reflect the growing data volume rather than true change. No fire danger index was computed, no trend was estimated, no formal trend or change-point test was applied, and the only treatment of trend was its removal before the comparison, so the paper supplies the caveat on gridded weather input rather than a trend result.
