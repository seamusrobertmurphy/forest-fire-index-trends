Question Q2, how the weather input of a fire danger index changes its level and its skill, because the paper computed the same indices from forecast and from observed weather and from daily averages against daily extremes and reported which input gave the more reliable index; it is weak for the review because the indices are the United States National Fire Danger Rating System and Fosberg indices, not the Canadian Fire Weather Index System, and the comparison covers one month.

Establishes Mölders, Weather and Forecasting 23(5), 953 to 973, ran thirty daily started five day Weather Research and Forecasting simulations at 4 km over interior Alaska for June 2005, a season in which 82 fires ignited in the model domain, and computed the Fosberg fire weather index, its fuel availability modification, the Keetch-Byram drought index and the National Fire Danger Rating System spread, energy release, burning and ignition components from the predicted weather and from observations at 29 sites, scoring them by bias, root mean square error, correlation, standard deviation of error and categorical skill at each forecast lead and for the ensemble. The model underestimated the diurnal amplitude of temperature and relative humidity, with daily maximum temperature 4.2 K too low and minimum relative humidity 12 per cent too high on the ensemble average, so indices built from daily averages scored better than those built from daily extremes, and the author concluded that indices from daily averages should be preferred until the extremes were predicted better, while noting that fire danger rating is meant to represent near worst case conditions at the peak of the burning period. Errors in the accumulated indices grew only marginally with time, the indices tracked the days on which many fires ignited, and the National Fire Danger Rating System indices exceeded the 29 site average at most ignition sites whereas the Fosberg indices added no value. The paper fitted no occurrence model, used no Canadian index, and estimated no trend, so it bears on the review only as evidence that the choice of weather input, extremes against averages, changes the level and the skill of a fire danger index.
