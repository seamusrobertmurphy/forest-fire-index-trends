Question Q2, how a change of instrument, observing practice or input has been handled as a homogeneity problem in a daily series, because the paper supplies a changepoint test and an adjustment method built for daily, skewed, autocorrelated station data of the kind a fire danger series is computed from.

Establishes Wang, Chen, Wu, Feng and Pu, Journal of Applied Meteorology and Climatology 49(12), 2416 to 2436, extended the penalized maximal F test with first order autocorrelation, PMFred, by searching a Box-Cox power transformation parameter over the interval from minus one to one so that the common trend two-phase regression test could be applied to nonzero daily precipitation amounts, which are positive and skewed. The method, transPMFred, tested the occurrence frequency series and the nonzero amount series separately, used the stepwise PMFred algorithm for multiple changepoints, kept the PMFred critical values with lag one autocorrelation accounted for, and held a minimum segment length of 10. A simulation study built 1000 homogeneous surrogate series by a five day block bootstrap of real Canadian daily precipitation, with 600 nonzero values for one shift and 900 for two, and found false alarm rates near the nominal 5 per cent and hit rate gains over the untransformed PMFred of up to 71, 54, 40 and 25 per cent for shifts of 0.5, 1.0, 1.5 and 2.5 standard deviations. Two adjustment methods followed, an inverse Box-Cox mean adjustment and a quantile matching adjustment that preserved the fitted linear trend and fitted a natural cubic spline to category mean differences, with the recommendation of at least 30 years of daily precipitation, or 15 years of a continuous variable, to estimate each segment's distribution. Applied to six Canadian stations from 1883 to 2008, the test found London and St. John's homogeneous and found two or three changepoints at Amos, Dawson, Alert and The Pas that matched the metadata, while the record at The Pas showed that a change in measuring precision produces a frequency discontinuity that no amount based adjustment can repair. The paper concerned precipitation homogeneity, not fire danger, and computed no trend in any index, but its changepoint test, its block bootstrap of daily series and its quantile matching adjustment are the methods a station fire weather record would be homogenised with.
