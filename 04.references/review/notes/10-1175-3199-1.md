Question Q1, because it introduces the Benjamini and Hochberg (1995) false discovery rate procedure to climatology as the correction for many simultaneous trend tests and shows by simulation that the procedure keeps its control on spatially correlated climate data, which is the correction the manuscript applies within each station's family of metrics.

Establishes Ventura, Paciorek and Risbey, in Journal of Climate 17(22), 4343 to 4356, set out the false discovery rate procedure of Benjamini and Hochberg (1995) as a replacement for testing each location at a fixed level or for the field significance test of Livezey and Chen (1983), using as the worked example the linear regression slopes of a winter temperature variance index against time over 1949 to 1999 at 3024 grid cells between 20 and 70 degrees north from Paciorek et al. (2002). At a 5 per cent level the three procedures flagged 941 locations by the traditional test, 19 by the Bonferroni correction and 338 by the false discovery rate, nested one inside the other, and the paper argued that the false discovery rate controlled the more useful quantity, the expected proportion of rejected hypotheses that were false, so that 338 rejections at q of 5 per cent implied fewer than 17 false positives on average. The simulation study bootstrapped detrended residuals from the same grid, resampling 51 years with replacement, to build data with no spatial correlation, with the correlation of the real field, and with stronger correlation, added linear trends at 0 to 99 per cent of locations at signal to noise levels of 10, 15 and 20 per cent of variance, and averaged over 10,000 datasets. The original procedure kept its false discovery rate below q at every proportion of true alternatives even on the correlated data, becoming more conservative as correlation rose, while the general procedure of Benjamini and Yekutieli (2001) was too conservative to be useful and the resampling procedure of Yekutieli and Benjamini (1999) gained little for 97 CPU seconds against 7 by 10 to the minus 5 seconds per field, so the authors favoured the original procedure for its simplicity. A modified procedure that estimated the proportion of true alternatives, 61.7 per cent in the example, tightened the control and raised the count of significant locations from 338 to 807. The paper used ordinary least squares slopes, not Theil-Sen or Mann-Kendall, treated spatial but not serial correlation, and tested no fire danger series.
