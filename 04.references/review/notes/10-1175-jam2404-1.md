Question Q1, because it supplies the multiple-testing correction, control of the false discovery rate of Benjamini and Hochberg (1995), that a set of trend tests across stations or metrics needs, and shows that the correction stays approximately valid when the tests are correlated, which the eight metrics at one station are.

Establishes Wilks, in Journal of Applied Meteorology and Climatology 45(9), 1181 to 1189, showed by simulation that controlling the false discovery rate across K local hypothesis tests also serves as a test of field significance, with the control level q numerically equal to the global test level, and that it outperforms the traditional field significance test of Livezey and Chen (1983), which counts the number of locally significant results. The simulations used K simultaneous two-sample t tests of a difference in means, each with 50 observations per sample, on synthetic data whose spatial correlation between neighbouring tests was set by a parameter ρ, with K of 20 for independent tests and 100 or 1000 for correlated tests, and 100,000 replications per setting. With K of 20 independent tests, the counting test was conservative, rejecting a true global null 1.6 per cent of the time at a nominal 5 per cent, and when only two of the twenty local nulls were false it rejected the global null in only 22.6 per cent of cases even where both false nulls were rejected with near certainty, while the false discovery rate and Walker tests held their nominal level and kept good power. With correlated tests the counting test became permissive, rejecting true global nulls more than 25 per cent of the time at a nominal 5 per cent for strongly correlated data with K of 1000, whereas the false discovery rate and Walker tests computed under the independence assumption became only modestly conservative, so a significant result under that assumption implied an even more significant one had the correlation been accounted for. The paper concluded that the false discovery rate approach should be used in preference to the counting test in most field significance calculations, and noted that its insensitivity to spatial correlation lets it be combined with any local test that handles temporal correlation correctly. No fire danger index, trend estimator or observed weather record was used, and the paper did not treat serial correlation within a local test itself.
