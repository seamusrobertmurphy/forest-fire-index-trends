Question Q2, how a change of instrument, site or input has been handled as a homogeneity problem, because the paper is the standard review of the changepoint tests a station series is screened with before a trend is estimated, and states that a trend is trustworthy only when the series is homogeneous and that autocorrelation ignored by these tests produces false changepoints.

Establishes Reeves, Chen, Wang, Lund and Lu, Journal of Applied Meteorology and Climatology 46(6), 900 to 915, reviewed, modified and compared eight changepoint detection methods for climate series under the assumptions of independent Gaussian errors and at most one changepoint, the standard normal homogeneity test of Alexandersson recast with a pooled variance, its nonparametric Wilcoxon rank sum analogue, the two-phase regression of Lund and Reeves allowing a mean and trend change, the common trend two-phase regression of Wang, a modified Vincent forward regression, a new backward generalised algorithm, and the Akaike and Sawa Bayes criteria. Simulation of 10000 series of length 100 under each of five regression models, with shifts of 0.5, 1 and 2 standard deviations at positions 50, 65 and 80 and trends of 0.005, 0.01 and 0.02 per step at a 5 per cent level, showed that the six level based tests held their 5 per cent false alarm rate, that the Akaike criterion gave a 70 per cent type one error with no changepoint and up to 91 per cent under a trend, that the standard normal homogeneity test and its rank version were most powerful for a pure mean shift when a reference series removed the trend, and that the common trend two-phase regression and the Sawa Bayes criterion were best when no reference series was available. Applied to annual temperatures at Tuscaloosa 1940 to 1986 all eight found the 1956 change one year late, and at Libby 1911 to 2000 they placed the documented 1938 change between 1930 and 1947 under three different models, with a lag one autocorrelation of 0.11 offered as one reason. The review stated that trend estimates are trustworthy only for homogeneous series, that the methods were built for annual series and would not work well on periodic or autocorrelated daily or monthly data without the extension of Lund et al. (2007), that positive autocorrelation left unmodelled produces too many false changepoints, and that reference series fail unless themselves free of changepoints. It estimated no trend, treated no fire index and gave no multiple testing correction beyond noting that ignoring the many candidate times yields too many false changepoints.
