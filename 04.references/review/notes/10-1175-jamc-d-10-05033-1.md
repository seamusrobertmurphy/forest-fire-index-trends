Question Q2, because it supplies a homogeneity method that finds several mean and variance shifts at once in a climate series tested against a reference, the kind of test a version or input change in a danger index series would need, and it states that the method assumes independent values and does not yet handle daily or autocorrelated series.

Establishes The authors, whose names the article page text layer did not carry, published in the Journal of Applied Meteorology and Climatology volume 51 issue 2 (2012), whose page range the page did not carry, a segmentation method named GAHMDI that fitted a left-to-right hidden Markov model with Gaussian states by the Baum-Welch and Viterbi algorithms, seeded by a genetic algorithm to avoid local maxima, and chose the number of segments by a two-part minimum description length criterion with a minimum segment of four steps. On 1000 pairs of simulated candidate and reference series of 100 independent Gaussian values, GAHMDI matched the Caussinus and Mestre method and the standard normal homogeneity test for a single shift of 0.1 to 1.5 standard deviations while RHtest underdetected, found all three of a set of three shifts 229 times against 57 for Caussinus and Mestre and 148 for RHtest, and found a variance change of 0.5 after the 50th value 77 times against 12, 10 and 4. On winter precipitation at Milan, 1950 to 2006, with one reference series, only a break in 1991 was flagged by every method. The paper worked on annual and seasonal series, assumed independence because detection ran on a candidate minus reference difference, tested no fire danger index and no daily series, and named autocorrelated and daily data as future work.
