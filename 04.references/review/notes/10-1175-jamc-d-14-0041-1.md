Establishes The authors, whose names the article page text layer did not carry, published in the Journal of Applied Meteorology and Climatology volume 54 issue 2 (2015), whose page range the page did not carry, a Bayesian max-stable hierarchical model after Reich and Shaby (2012) fitted to fire season maxima, 1 September to 30 April, of the McArthur forest fire danger index over Australia on a 0.2 degree grid of 17 363 sites from the 1958/59 to the 2011/12 season. The index was computed from SILO interpolated daily maximum temperature, relative humidity at that maximum and rainfall with the Keetch-Byram drought index, and from monthly mean wind speed from the MesoLAPS model rather than daily wind, so return levels were later scaled by exp(0.0234 times 30) to stand for a windy day. The site maxima had generalized extreme value margins with location, scale and shape given Gaussian intrinsic conditional autoregressive priors on latitude, longitude, their interaction and state indicators, spatial dependence through an asymmetric logistic structure with knots sampled at one tenth of the sites, and inference by 14 000 Markov chain Monte Carlo iterations after 6000 of burn-in, taking about 50 hours. The primary product was a map of 10 year return levels proposed as a spatially varying severity threshold in place of the flat national value of 75, lower in Tasmania, the east coast and the ranges and about 75 around Melbourne, with 90 per cent credible interval half-lengths of 2 to 6 units at the sites plotted. Fitting the model to each decade from the 1960s to the 2000s showed a recent rise in the southeast and at Melbourne with other sites flat, but the paper fitted no trend, applied no significance test to the decadal differences, used a daily maximum temperature index rather than any hourly input, and worked on the McArthur index rather than the Canadian system.
