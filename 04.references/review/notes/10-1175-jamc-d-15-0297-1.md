Question Q1, because the paper describes the Global ECMWF Fire Forecasting reanalysis from which the gridded Fire Weather Index series used in global and Canadian trend studies are drawn, with its weather input stated, and Q3, because it validated the index against observed fire occurrence by a discrimination diagram and a rare event skill score over a fixed period without testing any change in the relationship over time; it bears on Q2 only in that its noon input is a composite of three hourly model fields, not an observation at a fixed hour.

Establishes Di Giuseppe, Pappenberger, Wetterhall, Krzeminski, Camia, Libertà and San Miguel, Journal of Applied Meteorology and Climatology 55(11), 2469 to 2491, introduced the Global ECMWF Fire Forecasting model behind the European Forest Fire Information System and ran it from ERA-Interim for 36 years from 1 January 1979 at a daily time step on a 0.25 degree grid, computing the Canadian Fire Weather Index, the McArthur Mark 5 fire danger index and the National Fire Danger Rating System ignition component from the same forcings, with daily accumulated precipitation, minimum and maximum temperature and humidity from three hourly fields and the noon temperature, humidity and wind taken as a collage of the model output nearest 1200 local time. Indices were set to zero under snow or when daily precipitation exceeded 1.5 mm, the first year was discarded as spin up, and no observation entered the fire index itself. The three indices were normalised through their cumulative distribution over August 2000 to December 2013 and validated against GFED4 daily burned area for the 4900 days of that period, a fire day being a cell with burned fraction above 0.1, about 2500 ha, with the extremal dependency index computed on a 3 by 3 pixel neighbourhood. In about 80 per cent of cases the indices flagged fire events above and non events below their climatological median, a fire followed 60 per cent of the days in the upper quartile, skill was highest in boreal forest and the Fire Weather Index performed best there, with higher skill in the Canadian boreal shield west than in the montane cordillera, and the score fell by about 20 per cent when only fires under 2500 ha were counted. The paper estimated no trend, fitted no count model and used no station weather, and reported the reference distribution and the input hour from which the gridded series inherits its level and its tail.
