Question Q2, because it is the one study in the group that set a station record against a gridded one over the same 22 years and found the trend verdict depended on the input, and Q1 at the margin, for its use of the Hamed and Rao modified Mann-Kendall test with Benjamini-Hochberg control across months and percentiles; the index is the Hot-Dry-Windy Index, not the Canadian FWI.

Establishes Silver, in the thesis behind the Journal of Applied Meteorology and Climatology article by Silver, Kleiber and Lundquist (the saved text carries no journal pagination), computed the daily Hot-Dry-Windy Index, the product of vapour pressure deficit at 2 m and the fastest wind in the lowest 500 m, from the National Renewable Energy Laboratory M2 tower in Boulder County, Colorado, for 2002 to 2023, with 1 minute data filtered to a 2 minute sustained wind, and from hourly ERA5 at the nearest 0.25 degree grid point for 2002 to 2023 and 1957 to 2023, standardised each series, split it into trend, seasonal and random parts, and tested the trend part with the Hamed and Rao (1998) modified Mann-Kendall test at alpha 0.05, controlling the false discovery rate by Benjamini-Hochberg across the 36 monthly percentile tests. The tower showed no significant trend in either index form (p = 0.464 and p = 0.8388), whereas ERA5 at the same point over the same 22 years and over 67 years showed significant rises with p near zero, every August percentile rising, and the June 95th percentile rising over most of the state. ERA5 daily maximum wind followed a Weibull distribution with scale 6.149 m per second against 14.934 for the tower, a bias of -7.972 to -7.453 m per second that fell to -4.416 to -3.992 when the tower was averaged to hourly and remained when 2 km WIND Toolkit winds were averaged to the ERA5 cell, so neither temporal nor spatial resolution explained it. The author read the disagreement as a warning that reanalysis may misstate fire weather trends at a point in complex terrain and cited Weatherhead and others (1998) on the record length needed to detect a small trend. No Theil-Sen slope, no fuel moisture code and no fire occurrence record was used.
