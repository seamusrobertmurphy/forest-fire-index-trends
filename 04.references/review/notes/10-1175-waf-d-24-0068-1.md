Question Q2, because it builds an hourly fire weather index on the ground that existing indices, the Canadian FWI among them, give one value per day, and it reports that the hourly product tracks the diurnal cycle of fire activity but misses the peaks.

Establishes The authors, whose names the article page text layer did not carry, published in Weather and Forecasting volume 40 issue 9 (2025) a new hourly wildfire potential index (HWP) computed from the 3 km High-Resolution Rapid Refresh model over the western United States, fitted to hourly normalised fire radiative power from VIIRS and MODIS at nine training wildfires of 2018 to 2020 (1615 hourly points) and verified against the independent RAVE geostationary product at six wildfires of 2019 and 2020 (56 complete days). The index was the product of 10 m wind gust potential, 2 m vapour pressure deficit, one minus soil moisture availability and a snow term, with exponents 1.50, 0.73 and 5.10 found by nonlinear least squares, and it gave a Pearson correlation of 0.44 with normalised fire radiative power on the training set. Against a climatological diurnal cycle the HWP had the lower root mean square error through most of the day but underpredicted the magnitude of fire activity, especially the peaks of high intensity fires. The paper did not compute the Canadian FWI, did not compare an hourly with a once daily version of any index on an observed station record, estimated no trend, and modelled no fire occurrence count; its bearing on Q2 is the motivation that daily indices give one value per day and the finding that an hourly product captured the diurnal cycle of fire activity.
