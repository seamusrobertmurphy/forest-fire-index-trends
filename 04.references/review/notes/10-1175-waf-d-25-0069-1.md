Establishes The authors, whose names the article page text layer did not carry, published in Weather and Forecasting volume 40 issue 12 (2025), with the support of the BC Wildfire Service, a comparison of three global ensemble forecast systems (ECMWF ENS with 50 members, NCEP GEFS with 30, Canadian GEPS with 20) and the ECMWF deterministic HRES for forecasting the six components of the Canadian Forest Fire Weather Index System of Van Wagner (1987) over British Columbia and Alberta, 48 to 60 degrees north and 109 to 139 degrees west, for April to September of 2021 to 2023, verified against ERA5 reanalysis and, in an appendix, against 139 stations with continuous records. The indices were computed from local noon temperature, relative humidity, wind speed and 24 hour precipitation with the overwintering rules of Wotton and Flannigan (1993) and McElhinny et al. (2020), ERA5 moisture codes initialised each first forecast day, and a 1991 to 2020 ERA5 climatology as the baseline. Skill was scored by mean absolute error, the continuous ranked probability score and the area under the precision-recall curve for days with FWI of 19 or more, about 10 per cent of all events. ENS had the lowest error for every component at every lead time, ensemble mean and median forecasts beat HRES for FFMC, ISI and FWI at all leads, and HRES kept its advantage only for DMC to day 5 and DC to day 9 or 10; the HRES PR-AUC exceeded 0.79 in the first three days. The paper estimated no trend, modelled no fire occurrence, used only the daily noon FWI, and named no version change, so it serves Q1 only as a British Columbia gridded FWI record built on the 1991 to 2020 reference period and the FWI 19 spread threshold.
