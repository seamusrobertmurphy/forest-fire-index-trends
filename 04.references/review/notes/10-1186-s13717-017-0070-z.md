Question Q2, because it fixes the once-daily noon input of the standard FWI System and the separate hourly FFMC, ISI and FWI of the accessory fuel moisture system, and Q4 at the margin, because it is the reference implementation of the 1987 system that FWI2025 replaces and it predates that update.

Establishes Wang, Wotton, Cantin, Parisien, Anderson, Moore and Flannigan, Ecological Processes 6, 5, pages 1 to 11, described the cffdrs R package, which computed every output of the Canadian Forest Fire Weather Index System and the Fire Behaviour Prediction System, plus the sheltered duff moisture code, grass fuel moisture code, overwintered drought code and fire season start and end dates, and was tested against the official Canadian Forest Service test datasets. The standard fwi function took the once-daily noon local standard time observation of temperature, relative humidity, 10 m wind and 24 hour rain, which in Canada is standardised at 1300 h local daylight time, and carried the previous day's moisture codes forward, so that the outputs stood for the day's peak afternoon fire potential; the hffmc and hffmcRaster functions took hourly observations and the previous hour's FFMC to give an hourly FFMC and, with a daily BUI supplied, hourly ISI and FWI. The worked examples used two years of daily weather from Penticton, British Columbia, in 2013 and 2014 to show that the overwintered drought code shifted DC, BUI and FWI through the following season, and hourly 10 km rasters over Alberta. The paper was a software description with no trend analysis, no fire occurrence model and no comparison of hourly against daily index series, and it anticipated a next generation of the system without naming FWI2025.

Quotations

"These indices are unitless indicators of fire potential that are calculated based on the daily noon observation of fire weather, namely the screen-level air temperature and relative humidity, 10-m open wind speed, and 24-h accumulated precipitation collected at solar noon (in Canada this observation time is standardized at 1300 h local Daylight Savings Time)." (p. 2)

"The outputs of the system are indicative of maximum expected fire potential for the day, which occurs during the warmest part of the day, the mid- to late-afternoon." (p. 2)

"The standard FWI system tracks only daily levels of fuel moisture and fire behavior potential; however, because some elements of the fire environment vary significantly throughout the day, elements of the AFM system provide ways of adjusting some outputs (i.e., fine fuel moisture code (FFMC), initial spread index (ISI), and fire weather index (FWI)) at different times of day (Van Wagner 1977)." (p. 2)

"The fwi, fwiRaster, and sdmc functions calculate their once-per-day outputs based on daily noon local standard time (LST) weather observations of temperature, relative humidity, wind speed, and 24-h rainfall, as well as the previous day's moisture content." (p. 4)

"The hffmc, gfmc, and hffmcRaster functions calculate the outputs based on hourly weather observations of temperature, relative humidity, wind speed, and hourly rainfall, as well as the previous hour's moisture content as indicated by FFMC or GFMC." (p. 4)

"These functions have been tested against the official test datasets, which make the package an excellent reference for algorithms used in other systems or being adapted in other countries." (p. 4)

"Because of the slowness of drying in this particular fuel layer, this starting difference can potentially bias the outputs of the FWI system over an entire fire season." (p. 5)

"In a standard FWI system calculation, the initial spread index and consequently the fire weather index depend on FFMC (see Fig. 2) and thus, this hourly function also calculates an hourly value for the ISI and FWI (hISI and hFWI)." (p. 8)

"For the purpose of this example, we used a set value; however, these indices would typically be calculated from a daily weather record that carried on from the start of the season up to the start of the hourly calculations." (p. 8)

"The new features, especially the ongoing development of a next generation of the CFFDRS, will be documented and added to the online package as well as the Western Partnership for Wildland Fire Science website (http://www.ualberta.ca/~wcwfs/)." (p. 10)
