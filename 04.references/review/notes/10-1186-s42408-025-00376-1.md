Question Q3, what seasonal terms a daily occurrence model needs beside fire danger, and whether the danger to occurrence relationship changes within or across years.

Establishes Coogan, Cannon and Flannigan built a 2000 to 2020 day of year climatology of flashes per lightning fire for four ecozones and three British Columbia ecoprovinces, fitted it by linear and quadratic regression on day of year, Spearman correlation and random forest, and found that ignition efficiency in the Montane Cordillera and the Southern Interior rose through the summer to a peak in early August independent of the fuel moisture codes, which supports a day of year term beside fire danger. No interannual change was tested; the series is averaged over years. Fire Ecology 21, 34.

Quotations

"We created a climatology (2000–2020) of daily lightning efficiency (i.e., the ratio of cloud-to-ground lightning flashes to lightning-caused wildfires that occurred) over the meteorological summer for four ecozones and a subset of British Columbia (BC) ecoprovinces." (p. 1)

"We examined relationships between lightning efficiency, day-of-year, and the above variables using a combination of linear models, Spearman's correlations, and Random Forest (RF) regression." (p. 1)

"The model with DOY expressed as a quadratic variable (Lightning Efficiency ~ DOY + DOY2; adj. R2 = 0.5608; F(2,88) = 58.47, p = < 2.2e − 16) better fit the data than a univariate model (Lightning Efficiency ~ DOY; adj. R2 = 0.4428) based on adj. R2. The model predicted the highest efficiency to be 81 flashes per fire on 09 August." (p. 7)

"The DOY variable was consistently among the top three important drivers of lightning efficiency in RF models for ecozones and was the top variable in ecozone models where DC was removed. This may be because the DOY variable captures the influence of a number of unaccounted for environmental factors that change as the summer progresses." (p. 15)

"Lightning efficiency, and, thus, the probability of a lightning strike igniting a wildfire, changes over the summer and varies by region. Therefore, models predicting lightning-caused fire occurrence, or other similar applications involving lightning ignition, may benefit by accounting for seasonal lightning efficiency in addition to the traditional fuel moisture variables." (p. 1)
