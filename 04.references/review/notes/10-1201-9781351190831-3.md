Question. Q1.

Establishes. Hubnerova, Esterby and Taylor (chapter in Evaluating Climate Change Impacts, CRC Press, 2020) fitted generalised extreme value distributions with linear time trends in the location and scale parameters to monthly maximum Fire Weather Index at 861 British Columbia stations over 1970 to 2018, by month and region, and found the most stations with increasing location trends in May, July and August where the maximum Fire Weather Index was already high, with decreasing scale in some regions in August. The abstract gives no slope, no autocorrelation treatment and no multiple testing correction, and the chapter could not be obtained.

Quotations

"The data were drawn from records of FWI within the years 1970 to 2018 and from 861 stations in British Columbia, Canada." (Abstract)

"Separate for each station, parameters of the generalized extreme-value distribution with linear dependence on time in both location and scale parameters were fitted by the maximum likelihood method." (Abstract)

"May, July and August had the most stations with stronger increasing trends in location parameter of maximum FWI and this tended to occur in regions where maximum FWI was higher." (Abstract)

"In contrast, trends in the scale parameter of maximum FWI showed decrease in variability in some regions, particularly in August." (Abstract)
