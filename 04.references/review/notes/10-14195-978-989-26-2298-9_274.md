Question Q1, a trend in the count of days above a local percentile of a Canadian FWI System component from gridded weather, with the Mann-Kendall test and no stated autocorrelation or multiple-testing treatment, and Q2 at the margin, because the percentile threshold is fixed to a 1981 to 2010 reference period on one gridded input.

Establishes Giannaros and Papavasileiou, in a conference chapter in Advances in Forest Fire Research 2022, pages 1774 to 1779, counted the annual number of days on which the Daily Severity Rating of the Canadian Forest Fire Weather Index System exceeded the grid cell's own 95th percentile, taken over a 1981 to 2010 reference period, in the 0.25 degree GEFF-ERA5 reanalysis over Europe from 1 January 1980 to 31 December 2019, and tested the series in four sub-regions with the Mann-Kendall test and the Pettitt change-point test. The counts rose in all four sub-regions, significantly (p < 0.05) in the south-west, south-east and north-east, by 6.3, 4.3 and 10.9 days per decade, and not significantly in the north-west, 3.0 days per decade, p = 0.23; the Pettitt test placed change-points in 1991 for the north-east and 1997 for the south-west. Annual extreme-day counts correlated positively with FireCCI burnt area for 2001 to 2019 by Spearman rank correlation, more strongly in the eastern sub-regions. The chapter gave no slope estimator beyond the Mann-Kendall decadal trend, no autocorrelation correction, no false discovery rate correction across sub-regions or grid cells, and no Canadian data, and the results were described by the authors as preliminary.
