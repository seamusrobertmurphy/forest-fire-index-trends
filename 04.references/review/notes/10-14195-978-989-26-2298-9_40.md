Question Q2, how the input to a Fire Weather Index System component, here precipitation and the spring starting value of the Drought Code, changes the level of that component against field moisture, with no bearing on trend, on hourly against daily input or on FWI2025.

Establishes Hanes, Wotton, Woolford, Bourgeau-Chavez, Bélair, Martell and Flannigan, Advances in Forest Fire Research 2022, pages 246 to 256, compared the Drought Code of the Fire Weather Index System, computed from the nearest fire weather station and converted to volumetric moisture content, against water content reflectometry probes at 10 and 18 cm depth in seven plots at four boreal sites, Edson and Red Earth Creek in Alberta and Dryden and Chapleau in Ontario, over the fire seasons of May to September 2019 to 2021, and against the Canadian Land Data Assimilation System soil moisture at three depths. Fit was scored by the coefficient of determination, root mean square error, percent bias and Nash Sutcliffe efficiency on the daily series without any treatment of day to day autocorrelation. The Drought Code tracked the deeper organic layers well, best at the Chapleau mixedwood plot at 18 cm with a coefficient of determination of 0.83 and a Nash Sutcliffe efficiency of 0.70, and worst at the Chapleau aspen plot at 18 cm with 0.53 and minus 5.95, while the land surface model consistently underestimated organic layer moisture because it was calibrated to mineral soils. Missed precipitation events and wrong spring starting values moved the fit more than any other factor. The paper tested no trend, no fire occurrence and no version of the Fire Weather Index, and it serves the review only as evidence that the weather input, not the component's formulation, drives most of the error in a moisture code.
