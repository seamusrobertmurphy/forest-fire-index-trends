Establishes Lemus-Canovas, Resco de Dios, Grayson, Olmo, Campos, Fidalgo-Castanon, Daher and Doblas-Reyes, Research Square preprint rs-11052030 version 1, posted 23 September 2026 and not peer reviewed, took the August 2025 fires of the northwestern Iberian Peninsula, which set both the highest August mean Fire Weather Index and the largest August burned area of the 1985 to 2025 Copernicus ERA5 record for the domain, and reran the month under three spectrally nudged 9 km IFS-FESOM storylines, a 1950-like counterfactual, the present climate and a climate 2 K above pre-industrial, with five members each. The Canadian Fire Weather Index was computed daily by the cffdrs R package from 2 m temperature, relative humidity and 10 m wind at 12:00 UTC and daily precipitation, with a six month spin up from 1 January 2018, bias corrected to the ERA5-Land index by quantile delta mapping fitted on 720 summer days of 2018 to 2025, then converted to potential fireline intensity by the Palheiro et al. (2006) power law for Pinus pinaster and cut at the Tedim et al. (2018) suppression thresholds. On the 15 August peak the area above 10,000 kW per metre rose by 0.47 million hectares, 3.9 per cent, from the counterfactual to the present and by 0.92 million hectares, 7.6 per cent, under 2 K, and the area above 30,000 kW per metre, nearly absent in the counterfactual, rose by 0.40 and 1.72 million hectares; population and protected area exposure rose in step. The authors acknowledged a small positive residual bias after correction and that the index to intensity relation is empirical. No trend estimator, no station data, no fire occurrence model, no hourly input and no mention of FWI2025 appeared.
