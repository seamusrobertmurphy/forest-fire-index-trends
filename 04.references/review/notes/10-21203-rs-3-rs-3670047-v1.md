Question Q2, because it ran four homogeneity tests, Pettitt, standard normal homogeneity, Buishand and von Neumann, on seasonal maximum fire danger series before trend testing and attributed the breaks found to changes in data collection and gap filling, and Q1 as a method comparator, because it paired the Mann-Kendall test with Sen's slope on seasonal extremes at 15 stations without any serial correlation or multiple-testing correction.

Establishes Hossain, Khastagir, Gato-Trinidad, Anwar and Rasel, a Research Square preprint posted 30 November 2023 and not peer reviewed, computed the daily McArthur Forest Fire Danger Index at 15 Bureau of Meteorology stations in Tasmania from daily maximum temperature, relative humidity at maximum temperature, wind velocity, rainfall and three hourly wind, with records running from 1965 to 2019 at the longest and 1997 or 1998 to 2019 at the shortest, took the seasonal maximum of each series, tested normality by three tests and homogeneity by four tests at the 5 per cent level, and then applied the Mann-Kendall test and Sen's slope to each season. The Pettitt test found breaks at one station in summer, three in autumn, five in winter and one in spring, and the standard normal homogeneity test found 66.67, 53.33, 73.33 and 80 per cent of stations homogeneous by season; the authors attributed breaks to changes in the data collection method and to filled gaps. The Mann-Kendall statistic was positive at 9 of 15 stations in summer, 10 in autumn, 5 in winter and 11 in spring, and the table was headed as computed with no consideration of serial correlation; no p values for the trend test were tabled, no false discovery rate or other multiple-testing correction was applied, and the stations tabled in the results, such as 94008, do not match the station numbers listed in Table 1, which the text does not explain. The index is the Australian McArthur index, not the Canadian system, and the paper did not test fire occurrence.
