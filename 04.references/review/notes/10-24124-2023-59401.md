Subject. During 2021 and 2022, a master's thesis tested the daily Fine Fuel Moisture Code and Duff Moisture Code of FWI1987 against fuel moisture sampled in central British Columbia. It ran them as published and in three locally adjusted forms, beside remote sensing indices and a burn severity case from the 2017 Plateau Complex fire.

Establishes. At six locations near Prince George and Smithers, each with open, juvenile and mature stands, 6116 samples of foliage, fine woody debris and duff were collected and oven dried. Fine woody debris, twigs and branches 2 to 7 mm across, was compared with the Fine Fuel Moisture Code, and duff with the Duff Moisture Code. The abstract named three versions of the model and the Methods described four, because the third approach was split into Models 3 and 4. Model 1 ran the daily equations of Van Wagner and Pickett (1985), the version the 2025 report labelled FWI1987, on weather from the nearest BC Wildfire Service station. The six stations, Bear Lake, Hixon, Bednesti, Houston, Upper Fulton and Pine Creek, lay 3.21 to 27.3 km from the sites. Both codes took noon temperature, humidity and 24 hour rain, and the fine fuel code also took noon wind. Station weather came from the Pacific Climate Impacts Consortium. The thesis said the stations usually observed in the afternoon but gave no clock hour. For 2021, the station codes were provided, and for 2022 they were computed with code the thesis did not name. Model 2 kept the station weather but replaced the default start values, 85 and 6, with the first observed moisture of each stand. It also set the moisture limits in both equations to the observed daily extremes. The limits came from the same 2021 and 2022 observations the models were scored against. Model 3 kept Model 2's limits and start values and took temperature and humidity from in-stand loggers at 30 cm, with wind and rain from the station. Model 4, for 2022 only, also took wind and rain from in-stand sensors at about 2 to 2.5 m. The thesis did not say which hour of the in-stand record entered Models 3 and 4. No model used Van Wagner's 1977 hourly method, the diurnal tables, cffdrs-ng or any part of the 2025 System. None used the Drought Code, Initial Spread Index, Buildup Index or Fire Weather Index. Under Model 1, root mean square errors ran from 26.78 to 47.42 per cent for fine woody debris and from 59.95 to 85.35 per cent for duff. Each Model 1 row used 93 or 94 daily stand means in 2021, and 106 for duff and 128 for fine woody debris in 2022. Under Model 2, the errors ran from 27.20 to 47.42 per cent for fine woody debris and from 72.20 to 94.33 per cent for duff. Model 3 gave 30.20 to 44.93 per cent for fine woody debris and 59.95 to 158.09 per cent for duff. Model 4 gave 21.13 to 27.20 per cent for fine woody debris and 66.11 to 89.40 per cent for duff. The thesis judged that no model worked consistently well, and that a full revision of the FWI model or new models using in-stand data might be required. Its only Fire Danger Group source was the 2021 Vision report, GLC-X-26, and it predated the 2025 report. The thesis was inconsistent in places, so each number needs checking against its table before it is cited. Figures 19 and 20 printed one plot for Models 1 and 2, carrying the R² values of Model 2's Table 10. Figure 22 labelled its Model 4 panels 2021, while Table 12 said 2022 and repeated Table 11's 2021 sample sizes row by row. The page 62 text said 2021 duff estimates were lower than observed. Table 9's regression lines, with x estimated and y observed, put observed duff below the estimate in every duff row except 2022 mature stands. This held for any mean estimate from 100 to 300 per cent.

Bears on

- Introduction, "The report compared the Fine Fuel Moisture Code of each version with field samples on four days of June 1999 in the Northwest Territories [@cffdg2025fwi2025, pp. 19–20]." (qmd L2200). Does not bear. The thesis predated the 2025 report and tested one version, as its Conclusions said, "The FWI and its locally modified versions were tested in this research." (PDF p. 109, printed p. 109). It was a field test of FWI1987 alone, on daily stand means over two summers in central British Columbia.
- Introduction, "No published study had compared the two versions over a longer observed record." (qmd L2200). Does not bear, and the absence claim survives this text. Its versions were the 1987 daily equations and local recalibrations of them, "To test the accuracy of the moisture contents generated by the FWI model in the study region, four versions of the model used in this research." (PDF p. 37, printed p. 37). None was FWI2025 or a prototype of it, and the record covered the summers of 2021 and 2022. The text was a master's thesis with a supervisor and two committee members named on its title page, and its PDF printed no DOI. Whether a thesis in a university repository counts as a published study in this sentence was left open.
- Abstract, "At fixed input, the 2025 equations raised the mean Duff Moisture Code by 5.8 and the peak Drought Code by 60, towards drier fuel." (03.outputs/abstract.md, from the template at qmd L3800). Does not bear. The thesis computed no 2025 code and no Drought Code. Under the 1987 equations on station weather, duff errors ran from 59.95 to 85.35 per cent (Table 9, PDF p. 64, printed p. 64). Its text gave the 2021 direction as "In 2021, the estimated duff MC was generally lower than the observed MC, with a large discrepancy between the best-fitting and one-to-one lines." (PDF p. 62, printed p. 62). Table 9's regression lines put observed duff below the estimate in all three 2021 rows, the opposite direction. The thesis therefore cannot show whether a higher Duff Moisture Code would match central British Columbia duff better or worse.
- Abstract, "No study had separated the two changes or tested trends across both." (03.outputs/abstract.md). Does not bear. The thesis changed only the weather source, station or in-stand, under the 1987 equations, as in "In-stand temperature and humidity data were used in Model 3, combined with nearby fire weather station data for wind speed and precipitation to reduce errors caused by weather differences." (PDF p. 41, printed p. 41). It never changed the version and described no hourly computation.
