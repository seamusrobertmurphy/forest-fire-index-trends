Question Q2, because it documents the 1988 revision of the United States National Fire-Danger Rating System as a version change that the software handled by keeping both the 1978 and 1988 fuel model sets selectable, and because it fixes the system's once-daily observation at a standard observation time with a 24 hour precipitation window.

Establishes Donaldson and Paul, United States Department of Agriculture Forest Service, Southeastern Forest Experiment Station, General Technical Report SE-61, April 1990, 60 pages, wrote the user's guide for NFDRSPC, a personal computer program that computed the 1988 version of the National Fire-Danger Rating System of Burgan (1988) from observed or forecast station weather, replacing the mainframe AFFIRMS system of 1972 that had been revised in 1978 and 1988 to follow each revision of the rating system. The guide set out the station observation entry, one basic observation per day at a recorded observation time with a 24 hour precipitation duration and amount, the fuel stick moisture calculated by the Burgan (1988) method when missing and by Fosberg (1977) for forecasts within an hour of 2 p.m., and the forecast entry of precipitation in a 16 hour and an 8 hour period between standard observation times. Its Appendix 2 recorded the version change itself, that the 1988 update modified all 20 fuel models, adding minimum and maximum wind adjustment factors and a reservoir of dead fuel for drought response, and that the user could choose the original 1978 models or the 1988 revision, with both tabulated. The report is a software manual; it evaluated nothing, compared no index versions on an observed record, estimated no trend and modelled no fire occurrence. The text layer is scanned OCR without printed page numbers, so quotations cite PDF pages, and the fuel model tables are garbled.

Quotations

"This user's guide is an introductory manual for using the 1988 version (Burgan 1988) of the National Fire-Danger Rating System on an IBM PC or compatible computer. NFDRSPC is a window-oriented, interactive computer program that processes observed and forecast weather with fuels data to produce NFDRS indices." (PDF p. 7)

"The 1972 fire-danger rating system was installed on a mainframe computer in 1972. The primary purpose of the program was to compute fire-danger indices, but various other functions such as a message system were added to provide communications among fire control organizations and to archive observed fire weather data." (PDF p. 7)

"AFFIRMS has migrated across a number of mainframes since its original installation. It was revised in 1978 to accommodate the 1978 Revision of the National Fire Danger Rating System (Burgan and others 1977; Deeming and others 1977), and was again revised in 1988 to accommodate the latest revisions (Burgan 1988)." (PDF p. 7)

"As a result of these concerns, the 1988 NFDRS (Burgan 1988) has been installed on a PC with a window/menu-oriented user interface." (PDF p. 7)

"If the Observed 10-Hour Timelag Fuel Moisture is entered as missing, then the 10-Hour T/L Moisture will be calculated using the Burgan (1988) method. Otherwise, the entered value will be corrected for the age of the fuel sticks, if the age is known." (PDF p. 29)

"If the 10-Hour Timelag Fuel Moisture is entered as missing, and if the Forecast Valid Time is within 1 hour of 2 p.m., the forecasted 10-Hour T/L Fuel Moisture will be calculated using the Fosberg 1977 method. In all other cases of this entry as "M," the forecasted 10-Hour Moisture will be calculated with the Burgan 1988 method." (PDF p. 33)

"Precip. Duration Period 1 is for the first 16 hours, and Precip. Duration Period 2 is for the last 8 hours of the 24 hours between standard observation times." (PDF p. 33)

"Caution: If you modify any items of an existing station other than Station Name, Unit, Fire Weather Forecast Zone, Agency Code, Associated Station, Measured Woody Fuel Moisture, or Fuel Stick Date, the program will discard the carried items, (1,000- and X1,000-hour timelag fuels moistures, and herbaceous and woody fuel moistures) and restart calculation of moistures and indices as if you were starting a new station." (PDF p. 34)

"The changes required by the 1988 update to the NFDRS program (Burgan 1988) required modification of all 20 NFDRS Fuel Models. A second set of fuel models was created. In some cases, fuel loads were changed to improve the seasonal response, but the major changes involved adding a minimum and maximum wind adjustment factor and a reservoir of dead fuel to improve the drought response. The user can choose which set of fuel models to use: the original 1978 NFDRS models or the 1988 revision of those models. Both the 1978 and 1988 models are listed below." (PDF p. 45)
