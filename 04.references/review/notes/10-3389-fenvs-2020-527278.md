Question. Q1 by its absence of a trend, and Q2 through composite station records.

Establishes. Taylor (Frontiers in Environmental Science 8, 527278, 2020) decomposed the Fire Weather Index and the Monthly Drought Code by spectral analysis at nine stations in the Pacific Maritime, Montane Cordillera and Boreal Plain ecozones, from one second wind to a century of July Monthly Drought Code, removing long term trends before analysis, and found spectral peaks of 3.5 and 9 years at Saanichton and Prince George and 26 years at Prince George, while the century series were composites of successive station records (Prince George 1912 to 1945 joined to Prince George A 1946 to 2019). No trend is reported.

Quotations

"Spectral peaks were also observed in the Monthly Drought Code in the Montane Cordillera (Prince George) and Pacific Maritime (Saanichton) ecozones at 3.5 and 9-years, and at 26 years in the Montane Cordillera." (p. 7)

"The white noise hypothesis was not rejected for the Lost River/Nipawin (Boreal Plains ecozone) dataset, which was a composite of 3 nearby stations." (p. 7)
