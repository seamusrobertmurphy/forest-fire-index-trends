Question. Q1, as a Canadian estimator precedent on fire occurrence and season length rather than on a fire danger index.

Establishes. Ahmed and Hassan (Fire 6, 96, 2023) applied the Mann Kendall test with Sen's slope to human and lightning caused fire counts, area burned and season start, end and length by Alberta natural subregion for 1959 to 2021, with the standard variance of S and no stated treatment of serial correlation or of multiple tests, and found rising occurrence in all subregions but Alpine, up to 3.68 fires per year in the Central Mixedwood, and seasons lengthening by 1.1 to 1.7 days per year in three subregions. The paper computes no fire danger index.

Quotations

"We applied a non-parametric statistical test, i.e., Mann–Kendall and Sen’s slope estimator, for the patterns and magnitudes of the trends." (p. 1)

"The highest annual rate of significant increase was observed in CM (3.68 NOF/year), followed by LF (1.56 NOF/year) and UF (1.41 NOF/year)." (p. 10)

"Additionally, the length of the fire season was found to be significantly longer in three ecoregions (LBH, NM, and KU) when considering both the 1st/99th and 5th/95th percentiles, with the rates ranging from 1.1 to 1.7 day/year (Figure 5b)." (p. 12)

"In general, we found one to two days for both early start and delayed end of fire season, and subsequently two to four days longer fire season, in five ecoregions." (p. 15)
