Question Q2, because it reports trends in an hourly gridded fire danger index and shows that the trend differs by hour of day, so that the hour at which the index is sampled changes the trend found; and Q1 as a comparator for threshold-count and percentile trend metrics.

Establishes Harris, Mills and Brown, in the Proceedings of the 23rd International Congress on Modelling and Simulation, MODSIM 2019, pages 747 to 753, described the trends and variability of McArthur's Forest Fire Danger Index over Victoria, Australia, for 1972 to 2017 from VicClim Version 3, a bias-corrected 4 km, hourly gridded reanalysis dataset built with the Weather Research and Forecasting model, with drought factor from Australian Water Availability Project daily precipitation. The metrics were the 90th and 99th percentiles of the index, the annual number of days on which 10 per cent of the state exceeded an index of 25, decadal differences from the period mean, and the first and last day of each July to June year above that threshold. Fire activity, the annual number of fires and the natural logarithm of area burned for 1972 to 2014, was correlated with the annual 90th percentile index after linear detrending, giving r = 0.67 and r = 0.79 (p < 0.01, n = 42). The number of days above 25 averaged 66 per year for 1972 to 2002 and 94 per year for 2002 to 2017, and the six seasons above 100 days all fell after 2002. The summer 3 pm and 9 pm index rose from the early 2000s while the 3 am and 9 am index did not, so the trend depended on the hour sampled. The paper used no Theil-Sen estimator, no Mann-Kendall test, no autocorrelation treatment and no multiple-testing correction; trends were read from anomaly series, five-year running means and period averages, and the only test was a Pearson correlation.

"A new and unique high spatial (4x4 km) and temporal (1-hour) resolution bias-corrected homogenous 46-year gridded fire weather dataset developed for Victoria (Brown et al 2016) makes it possible to identify the trends and variability of FFDI." (p. 748)

"To analyse the relationship between fire activity and FFDI, the data were linearly de-trended to capture only the interannual relationship rather than the effects of congruent trends. Pearson’s correlation coefficient (r) was then calculated with statistical significance assumed at the 95% confidence interval (p<0.05)." (p. 748)

"The correlation coefficient between fire activity and 90th percentile FFDI are for number of fires r=0.67 (p<0.01, n=42) and for natural logarithm area burned r=0.79 (p<0.01, n=42)." (p. 749)

"Through the 1970s to the early 1980s there is a period of oscillating years between positive and negative anomalies, followed by an extended negative anomaly from 1984 to 2000. From 2002 until 2017 the FFDI anomaly was positive with the exception of the strong negative anomaly in 2010-11; this 12-month period had very much above average rainfall over all of Victoria except East Gippsland, and for the DJF period most of the state had its highest on record rainfall (Bureau of Meteorology 2018), leading to very low drought factors." (p. 750)

"There is large year-to-year variability in the Victorian mean 3pm DJF FFDI values compared to the Victorian mean 3am and 9am DJF FFDI values (Figure 4c). The five-year running mean 3pm and 9pm FFDI follow the broad inter-decadal pattern found in the annual anomalies, but this is not as evident in the 3am and 9am FFDI values. The Victorian 3am and 9am mean DJF FFDI has remained relatively unchanged from 1972-73 to 2016-17, lacking the upward trend found for most other temporal periods." (p. 750)

"We find from 1972-73 to 2001-2002 there is an average 66 days (Figure 6a). However, since 2002-03 through to 2016-17 the average has increased to almost 94 days per year. This suggests that on average there are an additional 28 days with an FFDI over 25 for 2002-03 to 2016-17 compared to 1972-73 to 2001-02." (p. 751)

"There are only six years (July to June) that have over 100 days with an FFDI>25 and these all occur between 2002-03 and 2016-17." (p. 752)

"Selecting one occurrence above a threshold, as in Figure 6 may not provide a definitive measure of fire season start and end. To provide greater insight, we calculate the number of days with an FFDI over 25 each year for the shoulder months (September and April) (Figure 7)." (p. 752)

"Diurnal differences in trend and variability were observed with the summer 3pm and 9pm FFDI values revealing much greater year-to-year variability and an upward trend since the early 2000s, and the summer 3am and 9am FFDI values less variable and no upward trend." (p. 753)
