Question Q2, because it is a direct test of how the timing of the weather input, local noon against daily aggregates, changes the trend of a Fire Weather Index exceedance count on an observed record; and Q1, because it reports a global 1980 to 2023 trend by Theil-Sen with the Hamed and Rao corrected Mann-Kendall test and Benjamini-Hochberg false discovery rate, the same estimator set the manuscript uses, with a noted overestimate in western Canada.

Establishes Matteo, Garnés-Morales, Moreno, Ribeiro, Azorín-Molina, Bedia, Di Giuseppe, Dunn, Herrera, Provenzale, Quilcaille, Torres Vázquez and Turco, arXiv preprint 2503.01818 (2025), not peer reviewed, compared the Copernicus ERA5 Fire Weather Index of Vitolo et al. (2020), computed from local noon weather, with four recomputations from daily aggregates of the same ERA5 hourly fields, over the globe at 1 degree for 1980 to 2023, on the annual count of days above the local 95th percentile of FWI. Slopes were Theil-Sen, significance the Mann-Kendall test with the Hamed and Rao variance correction, and multiple testing across the grid was controlled by the Benjamini-Hochberg false discovery rate; trend differences were tested on the difference series by the same procedure, after Santer et al. (2000). The noon baseline rose by 2.65 days per decade, 11.66 days over 44 years, about 65 per cent of the mean of 18 days, while every daily approximation overstated the slope, from 2.86 to 3.06 days per decade, an excess of 0.22 to 0.39 days per decade that was significant for all four, largest for the common pairing of daily maximum temperature with daily minimum relative humidity, which produced significant overestimates over 14.82 million square kilometres, and the combinations using minimum relative humidity added spurious positive trends in western Canada. Relative humidity was the input whose substitution moved the trend most, and the authors found that the bias accumulated over time rather than holding constant, so a mean bias correction would not remove it. The study is gridded reanalysis, not station data, and it compared a noon instant with daily aggregates rather than hourly against once-daily input; it did not name FWI2025.

Quotations

"Here, we examine how using daily approximations for FWI95d—the annual count of extreme fire weather days exceeding the 95th percentile of local daily FWI values—compares to the standard noon-based approach for the period 1980–2023." (PDF p. 1)

"Our findings reveal that FWI95d calculated with noon-specific data increased globally by approximately 65%, corresponding to 11.66 additional extreme fire weather days over 44 years. In contrast, daily approximations tend to overestimate these trends by 5–10%, with combinations involving minimum relative humidity showing the largest divergences." (PDF p. 1)

"Because midday local time corresponds to peak fire danger in boreal regions, the FWI formulation uses meteorological data recorded at local noon (Van Wagner, 1987; Vitolo et al., 2020)." (PDF p. 3)

"We calculated time series slopes via the Theil–Sen estimator (Theil, 1950; Sen, 1968). We then used the modified Mann-Kendall test for serially correlated data, including the variance correction proposed by Hamed and Rao (1998)." (PDF p. 6)

"We applied the False Discovery Rate (FDR) method (Benjamini and Hochberg, 1995) for multiple testing correction across the spatial grid." (PDF p. 6)

"Specifically, we (1) computed the difference time series (e.g., C1 – C0), (2) estimated the trend of this difference series using the Theil–Sen estimator, and (3) applied the modified Mann–Kendall test with the Hamed–Rao variance correction to account for serial correlation." (PDF p. 6)

"The global trend analysis (Figure 1) shows that the baseline FWI95d (C0) increased by 2.65 days per decade between 1980 and 2023." (PDF p. 7)

"Figure 1 also highlights that none of the daily approximations (C1–C4) preserve the C0 trend exactly, and all combinations overestimate the trend, ranging from 2.86 to 3.06 days per decade." (PDF p. 7)

"In Figure 1b, the daily approximations exceed the baseline trend by 0.22 to 0.39 days per decade, a difference statistically significant for all combinations." (PDF p. 7)

"In C3 and C4, additional positive trends appear in western Canada." (PDF p. 10)

"Figures 4 and S2, which isolate each substituted variable, confirm that substituting relative humidity exerts the strongest effect on FWI trends." (PDF p. 11)

"However, here we show that the differences introduced by daily approximations are not constant but accumulate over time, leading to overestimated projections of future fire weather risk." (PDF pp. 13 to 14)
