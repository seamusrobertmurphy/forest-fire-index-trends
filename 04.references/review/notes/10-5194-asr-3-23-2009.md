Question Q2, how a change of instrument or observing practice in a daily weather series has been handled as a homogeneity problem, with relative tests against neighbouring reference series and percentile-wise correction at the daily scale.

Establishes Štěpánek, Zahradníček and Skalák, Advances in Science and Research 3, 23 to 26, described the quality control and homogenisation applied to daily air temperature at 270 Czech climatological stations and daily precipitation at 787 stations over 1961 to 2007. Outliers were found by three methods together, pairwise difference series against up to eight neighbours within 300 km and 500 m of altitude, interquartile range limits, and comparison with spatially interpolated expected values, and the authors reported that no single method was adequate. Homogeneity was tested by the Standard Normal Homogeneity Test, the Maronna and Yohai bivariate test and the Easterling and Peterson test, against reference series built as inverse distance weighted averages of the five nearest correlated stations, on monthly, seasonal and annual series split into windows of at most 40 years with 10 year overlap, and a break year was accepted on the share of all possible test results that detected it, read together with station metadata. Breaks were corrected at the daily scale by a percentile-wise adjustment adapted from Déqué (2007), each month handled with its neighbours, and adjustments were withheld in any case of doubt. The automation of measurements from the late 1990s raised the count of temperature outliers and was the strongest influence on the homogeneity of the station series, which the staggered introduction of automation across the network let the authors detect and correct. The paper gave no fire danger index and no trend estimate; it bears on the review only as a documented daily homogenisation procedure for an instrument change in a station network, the same class of problem as a change of weather input to a fire danger index.

Quotations

"Quality control and homogenization has to be undertaken prior to any data analysis in order to eliminate any erroneous values and non climatic biases in time series." (p. 23)

"In this work, the use of various statistical tests and reference series made it possible to increase considerably the number of homogeneity test results for each series and thus to assess homogeneity more reliably. Inhomogeneities were corrected on a daily scale." (p. 23)

"This paper concentrates especially upon air temperature (270 climatological stations with the mean minimum distance of 13.3 km) and precipitation (787 stations with the mean minimum distance of 6.5 km) measuring at least 20 years in the period 1961–2007." (pp. 23 to 24)

"No one method alone was found adequate; only a combination lead to satisfying results – the discovery of real outliers and suppression of fault alarms." (p. 24)

"For air temperature, the higher number of outliers since the late 1990s coincides well with transition to automatic measurements." (p. 24)

"The relative homogeneity tests applied were: Standard Normal Homogeneity Test [SNHT] (Alexandersson, 1986, 1995); the Maronna and Yohai bivariate test (Potter, 1981); and the Easterling and Peterson test (Easterling and Peterson, 1995)." (p. 24)

"Reference series were calculated as weighted average from the five nearest stations (measuring within the same period as candidate series), with statistically significant correlations, a distance limit of 300 km and an altitude difference limit of 500 m." (p. 24)

"The tests were applied on monthly as well as seasonal and annual averages (sums in case of precipitation)." (p. 24)

"After evaluation of detected breaks and comparison with metadata, a final decision on correction of inhomogeneities was made. Data were corrected on a daily scale." (p. 25)

"Our process is based on comparison of percentiles (empirical distribution) of differences (or ratios) between candidate and reference series before and after a break." (p. 25)

"In the event of any doubt, the adjustments were not applied." (p. 25)

"Automation of measurements had very strong influence on the homogeneity of station time series (and even the occurrence of outliers). Fortunately, automation was introduced successively into the station network so it was possible to detect it and make corrections without major problems." (p. 26)
