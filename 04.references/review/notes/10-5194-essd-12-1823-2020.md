Question. Q2.

Establishes. McElhinny, Beckers, Hanes, Flannigan and Jain (Earth System Science Data 12, 1823 to 1833, 2020) computed the Fire Weather Index System from ERA5 for 1979 to 2018 twice, once overwintering the Drought Code and once restarting it at the default 15 each spring, validated the Fire Weather Index against Environment and Climate Change Canada station calculations for 1979 to 2009 with a mean Spearman correlation of 0.77, a mean absolute error of 5.0 and a mean bias of minus 3.7, and showed for 2016 over North America that the startup choice shifts early season Drought Code and Fire Weather Index and the count of spread days, chiefly in western North America where overwinter precipitation is low.

Quotations

"The second procedure sets the DC to its default start-up value (i.e. 15) at the start of each fire season." (p. 1823)

"We validate the FWI values over Canada using station observations from Environment and Climate Change Canada and find generally good agreement (mean Spearman correlation of 0.77)." (p. 1823)

"For regions where overwintering is likely to have an effect on spring fuel conditions, we therefore recommend using the version of the FWI calculation that overwinters the Drought Code." (p. 1829)

"This dataset shows that there can be a significant difference in DC (and therefore also BUI, FWI, and DSR) values, particularly at the beginning of the fire season, depending on which procedure is employed, suggesting that fire danger in some regions may be more severe than what is predicted by the default DC." (p. 1830)

"The FWI calculated from the ECMWF data shows generally strong agreement with calculations based on Canadian weather station data (mean Spearman correlation of 0.77, mean absolute error of 5.0, and mean bias of −3.7)." (p. 1830)
