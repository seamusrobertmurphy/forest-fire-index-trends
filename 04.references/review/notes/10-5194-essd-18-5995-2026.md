Question
Q2 and Q4.

Establishes
Benoit, Durand, Gachon, Boucher and Boulanger computed daily FWI System components for Canada from hourly ERA5 for 1950 to 2024, sampling the hourly fields at local solar noon instead of legal noon, and found that the sampling hour alone raises the July mean FWI by up to 3.4 units west of Alberta and leaves a step at every time zone boundary, while the daily correlation with the Canadian Wildland Fire Information System station FWI runs 0.70 to 0.86 under either method. The comparison is between two once-daily sampling hours, not hourly against daily, and no trend in FWI was tested. The saved PDF (21 pages, 10,902 words, published 25 August 2026) was searched and holds no mention of FWI2025, the 2025 update, NG-CFFDRS or CFFDRS2025; it follows Van Wagner (1987) with the overwintering of Hanes and Wotton (2024).

Quotations
"The ERA5-FWI-SN dataset is derived from the hourly ERA5 reanalysis using a new method, called the solar noon method, which is based on the sun’s elevation above the local horizon at noon." (p. 5995)

"Calculating FWI at local noon within each time zone produces a significant temporal bias between the easternmost and westernmost areas of the same time zone, since the same legal time is used regardless of the station’s location or where the meteorological data are collected." (p. 5996)
