Question Q2, how a change of weather input, here reanalysis against station observation at noon, shifts the level and the tail of the FWI and which input variables the index is most sensitive to, and Q1 at the margin, because the trend test is the Hamed and Rao modified Mann-Kendall test on the Seasonal Severity Rating, applied outside Canada.

Establishes Bedia, Herrera, Gutierrez, Zavala, Urbieta and Moreno, Natural Hazards and Earth System Sciences 12, 699 to 708, computed the Canadian FWI from the van Wagner and Pickett (1985) equations using noon values from three reanalyses, NCEP at about 2.5 degrees, ERA-40 at about 1.125 degrees and ERA-Interim at about 0.7 degrees, over the Iberian Peninsula, compared them with each other on the common 1989 to 2001 period by Mann-Kendall rank correlation, and validated NCEP and ERA-Interim against FWI computed from noon observations at seven Spanish AEMET stations with records from late 2005 or 2006 to June 2011. ERA-Interim correlated better with the stations than NCEP, with summer FWI tau above 0.5 at inland stations, and all reanalyses underestimated the observed FWI, NCEP most, with regression R squared of 0.86 for ERA-Interim against 0.65 for NCEP at Madrid. The share of reanalysis days above the 90th percentile that were also extreme at the station ran from 29 to 72 per cent for ERA-Interim and 20 to 51 per cent for NCEP, higher inland than on the coast. Decile binning showed the FWI most sensitive to relative humidity and temperature in Iberia, unlike the wind-led sensitivity reported for Australia. A trend test on the Seasonal Severity Rating from ERA-Interim for 1989 to 2011 used the Hamed and Rao (1998) variance correction for serial correlation and found a significant positive trend in summer tied to rising temperature and falling humidity, with no multiple-testing correction across grid cells. The study used daily noon input only, no hourly input, and did not compare index versions.
