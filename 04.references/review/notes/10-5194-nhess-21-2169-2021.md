Question Q1, as a trend comparator that found a small negative reanalysis trend in July and August peak Fire Weather Index for 1979 to 2017 whose interval included no change; and Q2, because it tested local noon input against daily mean and maximum input in one model and found the index values differed while the probability ratios did not, and because four reanalyses gave return times for the same event from 5 to 60 years.

Establishes Krikken, Lehner, Haustein, Drobyshev and van Oldenborgh, Natural Hazards and Earth System Sciences 21, 2169 to 2179, attributed the fire weather of the Swedish summer of 2018 by computing the Canadian Fire Weather Index from sub-daily data in four reanalyses, ERA-Interim, ERA5, JRA-55 and MERRA-2, and three large-ensemble climate models, EC-Earth, weather@home and CESM1, for Norrland, Svealand and Götaland. The event was the maximum seven-day running mean FWI in July and August, chosen because the correlation of monthly peak FWI from ERA-Interim with burned area for 1998 to 2017 was high from July onward and low in spring. Yearly maxima were fitted to a generalised extreme value distribution whose location and scale scaled exponentially with four-year smoothed global mean surface temperature, with 1000-member bootstrap intervals and the probability ratio as the measure of change. The return time of July 2018 was about 24 years in each region (90 per cent interval above 10 years), but the four reanalyses disagreed, from about 5 years in JRA-55 to about 30 years in the others for Norrland and from 8 to 60 years in Götaland, mainly through precipitation. The reanalyses showed a small negative trend in July and August FWI over 1979 to 2017 from falling wind and rising precipitation against rising temperature, with an interval that easily included no change and a trend largely absent in the 25-year longer JRA-55, so the authors drew no conclusion from reanalysis trends. The models gave a probability ratio of 1.1 (0.9 to 1.4) for the present and about 2 (1.5 to 3) at 2 degrees of warming. The paper used no Theil-Sen or Mann-Kendall estimator and no station observations, and modelled no daily fire occurrence.

"For EC-Earth, we compute the FWI based on local noon data (12:00 UTC, universal co-ordinated time), but for the CESM-LE (CESM Large Ensemble) and W@H sub-daily data are not available. Hence, for these models, we compute the FWI based on daily average wind speed and humidity, daily maximum temperature and daily cumulative precipitation. While this approach is common (Abatzoglou et al., 2019), results can differ between both methods, especially for fire danger extremes (Herrera et al., 2013). In order to assess whether this has an effect on our analysis, we tested the influence of using local noon data or the daily average combined with maximum temperature for EC-Earth. Though the values of the FWI do differ, there are no significant differences for both methods on the calculated probability ratios." (p. 2172)

"The reanalysis data show a slightly decreased probability of high-FWI events for all three regions for the 1C climate. This is due to a negative trend of July and August FWI over recent decades for these regions. A trend analysis of the FWI input variables during high-FWI events reveals a negative trend in wind speed, a positive trend in local noon surface temperature and a positive trend in 30 d cumulative precipitation prior to high-FWI events (not shown). The resulting net effect on the FWI is a small negative trend. However, the very large uncertainties easily encompass one (no change), indicating that this can also be a spurious trend caused by natural variability. The trend in July and August FWI is largely absent in JRA-55, which has a time span that is 25-years longer than the other reanalysis data sets. Hence, it is difficult to draw robust conclusions from the trends of the reanalysis data set alone." (p. 2174)
