Question Q2, in a minor way, because it is a percentile threshold study of a Canadian system component, the Daily Severity Rating at its 90th percentile, computed from ERA5-Land at 12:00 UTC with the Van Wagner and Pickett (1975) equations, and shows the threshold's level varies by place; it does not bear on hourly input, version change or trend.

Establishes Calheiros, Benali, Pereira, Silva and Nunes, Natural Hazards and Earth System Sciences 22, 4019 to 4037, computed the Daily Severity Rating of the Canadian Forest Fire Weather Index System from ERA5-Land at 0.1 degree, using 12:00 UTC temperature, relative humidity, wind and accumulated precipitation, for 2001 to 2019 over mainland Portugal, and attached to each of 2016 wildfires larger than 100 ha between 15 May and 31 October, 92 per cent of burnt area, the maximum Daily Severity Rating percentile over the fire's area and duration, dated by MODIS after Benali et al. (2016). Nationally the logarithm of accumulated burnt area rose linearly with the percentile (R squared 0.94), fires on days between the 85th and 95th percentiles made more than 80 per cent of total burnt area, only 6 per cent of burnt area fell below the 80th percentile and 12 per cent below the 90th, which the authors took as support for the 90th percentile threshold already used in Portugal. At the scale of 175 municipalities with more than three fires and more than 500 ha burnt, the percentile at which 90 per cent of burnt area accumulated ranged from 60 to 70 in the north eastern hinterland to between 85 and 95 on the coast, and complete linkage clustering on the correlation distance at a threshold of 0.35 gave nine clusters, whose forest or shrubland burnt area share separated them by a chi squared test (21.175, p about 4 times 10 to the minus 6) and Cohen's kappa of 0.3828. The paper estimated no trend, fitted no occurrence count model, used no station data and no hourly input, and did not test whether the threshold or the relationship changed over the 19 years.
