Question Q3, because it modelled satellite fire counts against Canadian Fire Weather Index System components outside Canada, at a monthly step with a leave-one-month-out hindcast, and therefore shows what a count model looks like without a count distribution, a seasonal term or a test of change over time.

Establishes Barry, Badiane, Sall, Diakhaté and Senghor, Environmental Management and Sustainable Development 8(2), pages 18 to 41, regressed monthly MODIS Aqua and Terra active fire counts and MCD45 burned area at 32 sites in Guinea, January 2003 to December 2013, 132 months each, on pairs of components of the Canadian Fire Weather Index System taken from the Global Fire Weather Database version 1p5 of Field et al. (2015) on a 0.5 by 0.66 degree grid by nearest neighbour. The model was a stepwise multiple linear regression on untransformed monthly counts, with terms kept at the 0.05 level and skill measured by a leave-one-out cross validated hindcast at lag 0 and lagged from one to five months. Explained variance for active fires ran from 29 per cent at Siguiri to 82 per cent at Koundara and for burned area from 16 per cent at Labé to 79 per cent at Kankan, all at p < 0.0001 by the authors' tables, with no burned area skill at Fria or Yomou; on the national monthly mean the Fire Weather Index alone explained 82 per cent of active fire variance and the Fine Fuel Moisture Code 50 per cent of burned area, and the Fire Weather Index with Drought Code predicted December fires up to three months ahead. No Poisson or negative binomial distribution, no harmonic or month term, no autocorrelation treatment and no multiple-testing correction were used, the fire season was a dry season of November to May with no fires detected in June to October, the index version and its input time were those of the Global Fire Weather Database rather than station weather, and the relationship was not tested for change over the 11 years. The abstract's ranges of 16 to 79 and 29 to 82 per cent do not match the ranges of 28 to 79 and 29 to 98 per cent stated in the results text, and the paper does not reconcile them.

A statistical analysis based on a multi-linear regression model was used to estimate the skills of FWI components on the predictability of burned area and active fire." (p. 19)

"The FWI components used are the Global FWI v1p5 of the fire weather index and they are available online at ftp.nccs.nasa.gov or on the website of the Center for research in climate and society at the University of Colombia (http://iridl.ldeo.columbia.edu/SOURCES /.GISS/.GlobalFWI/).
