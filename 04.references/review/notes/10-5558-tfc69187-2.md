Subject. The paper concerned the start and end of the fire weather season, the rule the Canadian Forest Fire Danger Rating System used to begin the fuel moisture calculation, and how a doubled carbon dioxide climate would lengthen the season.

Establishes. Wotton and Flannigan took daily weather from the Canadian Climate Centre general circulation model at 61 grid points in forested Canada under one and two times carbon dioxide, and daily records of 41 Atmospheric Environment Service stations over 1960 to 1989. They quoted the Canadian Forest Fire Danger Rating System start rule of Turner and Lawson (1978) in full, three consecutive days of noon temperature above 12 degrees Celsius where there was no snow, or three consecutive days without snow where snow was significant, and stated that the rule started the fuel moisture calculation and did not define the fire season. They defined their own season as starting after three days of maximum temperature above 12 degrees Celsius and ending after three consecutive days below 5 degrees Celsius. Under doubled carbon dioxide the season lengthened by 22 per cent or 30 days across Canada and by 51 days or 39 per cent in British Columbia, zone one. The paper did not compute any fire weather index and did not test the season rule against fire occurrence.

Bears on

- Weather Record, Season Window, "The window was fixed by calendar in every season" (qmd L2551). The sentence gave the Canadian start rule as three days after the snow cover disappeared. Wotton and Flannigan quoted the rule of Turner and Lawson (1978) with two branches, three consecutive days of noon temperature above 12 degrees Celsius where there was no snow, and three consecutive days of no snow where snow was significant. The sentence stated the second branch only, so the paper adds the first and corrects the summary where the stations lie in snow-free ground.
- Weather Record, Season Window, "The window was fixed by calendar in every season" (qmd L2551), and the script comment at qmd L1352 that read "three days above 12 degrees Celsius where snow cover cannot be observed". The paper placed the 12 degree branch in "areas with no snow cover", not where snow cover could not be observed, so the comment's gloss of the rule is wrong and the paper corrects it.
- Weather Record, Season Window, "The window was fixed by calendar in every season" (qmd L2551). The paper stated that the rule started the fuel moisture calculation and did not define the fire season, and that the System had no official end to the calculation. This supports the manuscript's choice of a fixed window with a separate spin-up start, and gives the paper's own operational season definition, 12 degrees Celsius to start and 5 degrees Celsius to end, both on maximum temperature.
- Introduction, "Fire danger has been rated throughout Canada since 1970" (qmd L2188), the clause "From one observation a day, taken at 13:00 local daylight time". The start rule the paper quoted was written on "noon temperatures", which is consistent with the noon local standard time observation the System specified, and supports the clause.
- Introduction, "In Canada over 1959 to 2015" (qmd L2190), the clause on the North Okanagan projection of a 30 per cent longer fire season. Wotton and Flannigan gave the earlier national projection, 22 per cent or 30 days across Canada and 39 per cent or 51 days in British Columbia under doubled carbon dioxide, which adds to the sentence.
- Discussion, "The study covered only the Canadian part of one basin" (qmd L3618), the clause "The fixed window from 1 May to 30 September could not register a season that lengthened at either end". The paper is the source of the projected lengthening in British Columbia, split evenly between spring and fall, and supports the limitation as stated.
