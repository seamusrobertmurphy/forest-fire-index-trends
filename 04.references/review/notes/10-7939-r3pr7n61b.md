Question Q2, because it measures how a change in the precipitation input, gridded analysis or interpolation in place of the gauge, moves each Fire Weather Index System code, with the slow-drying codes carrying an input bias for the rest of the season; it is weak for the review because it covers one and a half seasons and reports no trend.

Establishes Cai, in a Master of Science thesis at the University of Alberta, Department of Renewable Resources, 2017, compared the Canadian Precipitation Analysis System with 17 interpolation algorithms of five methods, inverse distance weighting, smoothed and unsmoothed thin plate splines, ordinary kriging and regression kriging with CaPA as covariate, for estimating 24 hour precipitation at Alberta Agriculture and Forestry fire weather stations recording at 1200 Local Standard Time, over 14 July to 31 August 2014 (49 days, 100 stations) and 1 May to 31 August 2015 (124 days, 81 stations), by leave-one-out cross-validation scored by mean absolute error, bias, equitable threat score and frequency bias index with 1000 bootstrap replicates. CaPA ranked 13th of 18 overall and second inside Doppler radar range; regression kriging with CaPA as covariate had 19.6 per cent lower mean absolute error than inverse distance weighting and became the best method above 0.6 stations per 10 000 square kilometres, CaPA below it. Fed into the FWI System with observed temperature, humidity and wind, the fast-drying FFMC, ISI and FWI favoured the lowest-error precipitation while the slow-drying DMC, DC and BUI favoured the lowest-bias precipitation, because an underestimated rain event early in the season was carried in DMC and DC to its end, and using the previous day's observed codes removed that propagation. The thesis estimated no trend, modelled no fire occurrence and compared no index versions or hourly input.

"To compare the performance of CaPA and interpolation methods in the forested area of Alberta, I evaluated precipitation estimates from CaPA and 17 algorithms of five interpolation methods, including IDW, smoothed TPS, non-smoothed TPS, ordinary kriging, and regression kriging. Precipitation estimates were generated using station observations through leave-one-out cross-validation and were evaluated using a range of skill scores (MAE, Bias, ETS, and FBI)." (p. ii)

"Results show that CaPA was only a mid-tiered method (13th of 18), except in Doppler radar covered areas, where CaPA performed second best. Regression kriging (with CaPA as a covariate) was the best method and produced precipitation estimates with 19.6% lower MAE compared with IDW." (p. ii)

"Additionally, this study showed that the FWI System responded to precipitation estimates differently due to their varying drying time lag of the indexes. Quick drying indexes (FFMC, ISI, FWI) preferred methods with lower MAE (e.g., regression kriging with smoothing), while slow drying indexes (DMC, DC, BUI) preferred methods with lower Bias (e.g., regression kriging without smoothing)."
