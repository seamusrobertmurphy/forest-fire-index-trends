Subject. The operational tables and observing instructions of the fourth Canadian danger rating system, the Ontario version of the 1956 tables that the Fire Weather Index replaced in 1970.

Establishes. The text was the 1956 edition of the Forest Fire Danger Tables for Ontario, a set of four tables giving a Drought Index to 25 and a Danger Index from 0 to 16 from noon rain, relative humidity and wind, with no temperature. It required every observation at sun noon, which it tabulated by place from 12:10 p.m. at Pembroke to 1:00 p.m. at Fort William in Eastern Standard Time, with one hour added under daylight saving and a two hour tolerance. Rain falling at noon postponed the observation, and rain past 2 p.m. cancelled the day. Up to three missed days were filled from the nearest station, and more than three restarted the record. Records began on the third day after the snow had cleared enough for fires to run in the open, at a Danger Index of 8 and a Drought Index of 3. The fall period began on September 1. Wind was estimated by its effects at a well exposed open place. The text is tables and instructions only, with no evaluation and no reference list.

Bears on

- Introduction, "From one observation a day, taken at 13:00 local daylight time" (qmd L2186-2225). Adds precision. The rule the Fire Weather Index inherited was sun noon by longitude, 12:10 to 1:00 p.m. standard time across Ontario, plus one hour under daylight saving. A fixed clock hour of 13:00 daylight time is the later simplification of that rule.
- Fire Danger Systems, "Its inputs were air temperature, relative humidity, wind speed at ten metres in the open and rainfall over the previous 24 hours" (qmd L2459-2468). Adds history. The 1956 Ontario tables used rain since the last observation, relative humidity and wind only, with no temperature and with wind estimated from its effects rather than measured at a standard height.
- Methods, "It replaced the Canadian start rule, which began the calculation three days after the snow cover disappeared" (qmd L2551). Supports it and gives the origin. The 1956 tables started on the third day after the snow had cleared enough for fires to run in the open, at a Danger Index of 8 and a Drought Index of 3.
- Study Design, "All three ran on the same weather, season window, startup values and gap filling" (qmd L2469-2476). Adds a precedent. The 1956 instructions filled up to three missed days from the nearest station and restarted the record after more, and a day with rain past 2 p.m. carried the previous day's index forward.
- Methods, "The window was fixed by calendar in every season, 1 May to 30 September" (qmd L2551). Adds to it. The 1956 tables changed from the spring and summer period to the fall period on a calendar date, September 1.
