Subject. The overwinter adjustment of the Drought Code of Turner and Lawson (1978), its carry-over and precipitation effectiveness fractions, tested against forest floor moisture sampled before and after three winters in Alberta.

Establishes. Anderson and Otway cored the forest floor of a mature white spruce stand in Elk Island National Park, 35 km east of Edmonton, before and after three winters, 2000 to 2003. They related the change in humus moisture equivalent to overwinter precipitation under the Turner and Lawson (1978) scheme. A regression gave a carry-over fraction of 1.3414 and a precipitation effectiveness fraction of 0.9074, with an r2 of 0.1681, rounded to 1.0 and 0.9. The park used 0.75 and 0.75 and the province 1.0 and 0.5. The fall Drought Code before the first snow of 2000 was 536, and overwinter precipitation ranged from 47.9 to 231.2 mm. Calculations resumed on 25 April 2001, 13 April 2002 and 21 April 2003 under a start rule of three successive days at 12 °C or warmer. A new curve for this stand gave a maximum humus moisture content near 140 per cent. The text tested no British Columbia station and said nothing about observation time or the hourly codes.

Bears on

- Lines citing the 2025 report elsewhere, "The window was fixed by calendar in every season, 1 May to 30 September, 153 days, with the calculation started on 1 April." (qmd L2551). The text recorded the Alberta start rule as three successive days at 12 °C or warmer, with starts on 25 April 2001, 13 April 2002 and 21 April 2003. A fixed 1 April start ran the codes before the operational start in all three years, so the text supports the sentence and adds the dates it replaced.
- Lines citing the 2025 report elsewhere, "It replaced the Canadian start rule, which began the calculation three days after the snow cover disappeared" (qmd L2551). Adds. The Drought Code at the start of a season was not a fixed value but an overwintered one, and the park and the province applied different fractions to the same stand.
- Introduction, table of expectations, row "Mean Duff Moisture Code and peak Drought Code", "the startup value moved the early season Drought Code [@mcelhinny2020era5]". Supports with field data. The fitted fractions differed from both operational choices and the fit had an r2 of 0.17, so the spring Drought Code depended on a choice the field data could not settle.
- Introduction, "The Fine Fuel Moisture Code (FFMC) tracks the surface litter, the Duff Moisture Code the loosely packed duff beneath it and the Drought Code (DC) the deep compact organic layer." (qmd L2186-2225). Supports, and adds the Drought Code's time lag of 53 days, corrected from the documented 52, its 100 mm water capacity and 18 cm nominal depth.
- Fire Danger Systems, "Each code was updated daily from its previous value with no reference period." (qmd L2459-2468). Adds. The previous value at the start of a season was computed by the overwinter scheme from the last fall value and the winter precipitation, not observed.
