Subject. The text concerned the Fire Weather Index System values reached during large boreal fires in Canada and whether they trended over 1959 to 1999.

Establishes. Amiro and co-authors described the fire weather of 9,333 fires larger than 2 km² in Canada's boreal and taiga ecozones, 1959 to 1999. They computed the indices from noon local standard time observations at 415 Environment Canada hourly stations, an Ontario fire weather archive and 90 daily rainfall stations. The values were interpolated to each fire by thin-plate spline and the highest daily value in its first 21 days was kept. National means of these maxima were a Fine Fuel Moisture Code of 91.4, a Fire Weather Index of 28 and a Drought Code of 298. Most indices were lowest in the eastern ecozones. Regressions on year showed no consistent trend in the indices, although fire numbers and area burned had risen. The paper named no province's stations, computed no hourly values and reported no homogeneity check.

Bears on

- Introduction, "In Canada over 1959 to 2015, area burned and the number of large fires rose significantly" (qmd L2186-2225). The paper added an earlier boreal result with the opposite finding for weather. Over 1959 to 1999 fire numbers and area burned rose, yet the authors "cannot detect consistent trends in weather indices associated with these large fires" (PDF p. 3).
- Introduction, "All of them predated the 2025 update" (qmd L2186-2225). The paper supported it, computing the indices under Van Wagner (1987) from noon observations, so its null trend was a statement about the 1987 System.
- Introduction, "From one observation a day, taken at 13:00 local daylight time" (qmd L2186-2225). The paper supported the hour in standard time, using "noon-hour (local standard time) values" (PDF p. 1) of temperature, humidity and wind.
- Fire Danger Systems, "Its inputs were air temperature, relative humidity, wind speed at ten metres in the open and rainfall over the previous 24 hours" (qmd L2459-2468). The paper supported the four inputs, with "24-h total precipitation" (PDF p. 1). It gave no anemometer height.
- Introduction, "From these it computes the Initial Spread Index (ISI), the Buildup Index and the Fire Weather Index (FWI), a single rating of fire danger." (qmd L2186-2225). The paper supported the phrase, saying the Fire Weather Index "is often used as a single integration of fire weather" (PDF p. 2).
- Introduction, "The time lag is the time the litter takes to lose most of the difference" (qmd L2186-2225). The paper added the slower codes' time constants under FWI1987, about 12 days for the Duff Moisture Code and 52 days for the Drought Code. Its value for the Fine Fuel Moisture Code was unreadable, printed in the layer as "213 days".
- Seasonal Metrics, "A Fine Fuel Moisture Code of 91 was taken from section 4.1" (qmd L2571). The paper added context for both absolute thresholds. The national mean of each large fire's highest code in its first 21 days was 91.4, and of its highest Fire Weather Index 28. Thresholds of 91 and 30 therefore sat near the typical peak conditions of large boreal fires.
- Seasonal Metrics, "The seasonal severity rating was the sum of the Daily Severity Rating over the season." (qmd L2569). The paper supported the metric's meaning, describing the rating as "a power function of FWI representing a measure of control difficulty for a fire" (PDF p. 2).
- Fire Danger Systems, "A danger trend here was therefore a trend in the weather rated against one fixed fuel." (qmd L2459-2468). The paper supported it, holding head fire intensity to the C-2 boreal spruce fuel. It warned of fuel-driven trends "that we would not pick up in our analysis" (PDF p. 3).
- Introduction, "Climate science treated such changes as a question of homogeneity" (qmd L2186-2225). The paper added a fire science example without that step. It merged three networks, 415 Environment Canada hourly stations, an Ontario fire weather archive and 90 daily rainfall stations, and reported no homogeneity test before its trend regressions.
- Introduction, "Over North America from 1979 to 2015 in a reanalysis" (qmd L2186-2225). The paper added an earlier null on a different quantity, each fire's 21-day maximum rather than seasonal counts. It found no consistent trend because of "large variation among years" (PDF p. 2).
- Introduction, "A danger rating system was judged on whether its outputs tracked fire occurrence." (qmd L2186-2225). The paper added a caution that conditions during large fires were shaped by suppression. Ecozone differences "likely also reflect fire management policies" (PDF p. 2), since southern fires escaped only under extreme conditions.
- Introduction, "Its terrain was steep enough that a station record was preferable to a reanalysis" (qmd L2186-2225). The paper added a contrast in method, interpolating station weather and indices to each fire location with a thin-plate cubic spline (Flannigan and Wotton 1989).
