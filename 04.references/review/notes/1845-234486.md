Subject. The spring start of the Drought Code, the standard starting value of 15 on the third day after snow loss, and the Turner and Lawson (1978) overwinter adjustment as applied to 194 stations in 1981.

Establishes. Alexander, at the Northern Forest Research Centre in February 1982, set out the standard procedure for starting the Drought Code in spring. Calculations began on the third day after an area was essentially free of snow, with a Drought Code of 15 assuming saturation and three days of moderate drying. Where the fall code was high or winter precipitation below normal, the Turner and Lawson (1978) adjustment applied. It took the last fall code, overwinter precipitation, a carry-over fraction of 1.00, 0.75 or 0.50 and an effectiveness fraction of 0.50, 0.75 or 0.90. In March 1981 the Centre issued look-up tables for 194 stations with 0.75 and 0.50. Thirteen per cent of stations started at 15, and the highest starting values were 272 at Norman Wells and 278 at Pelly Farm. Noon local standard time temperature was approximated as the daily maximum less 3 °C, and 24-h rain had to reach 2.9 mm to lower the code. The text covered no British Columbia station and no test against fire activity.

(qmd L2551). Supports, with the 1982 wording "on the third day after an area is essentially free of snow". Adds that the standard spring Drought Code was 15, raised by overwinter adjustment where recharge was incomplete.
- Lines citing the 2025 report elsewhere, "with the calculation started on 1 April" (qmd L2551). Adds a caveat. An error in the Drought Code starting value was carried through a good portion of the season until substantial rain, so a fixed start at a default value mattered most after a dry winter.
- Introduction, table of expectations, row "Mean Duff Moisture Code and peak Drought Code", "the startup value moved the early season Drought Code [@mcelhinny2020era5]". Supports. In 1981 the start values across 194 stations ranged from 15 to 278.
- Introduction, "From one observation a day, taken at 13:00 local daylight time" (qmd L2186-2225). Supports. The text's reference time was noon local standard time, the same hour, and it approximated that noon temperature as the daily maximum less 3 °C, a conversion in the opposite direction to pathway C.
- Introduction, "Their rain thresholds of 1.5 mm and 2.8 mm were applied to rain accumulated within a rain event" (qmd L2186-2225). Supports. The text stated the Drought Code rule as rain reaching at least 2.9 mm to have any effect, the same boundary stated from the other side.
- Study Design, "All three ran on the same weather, season window, startup values and gap filling, so no difference came from the record itself." (qmd L2469-2476). Adds. The text's open question about year-on-year carry-over of overwinter adjustments shows that start values were a recognised source of dependence between seasons in the operational record.
