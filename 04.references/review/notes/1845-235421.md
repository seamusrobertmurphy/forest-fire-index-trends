Subject. The report documented the structure, equations and fire data of the Canadian Forest Fire Behavior Prediction System, the fire behaviour part of the Canadian Forest Fire Danger Rating System.

Establishes. In 1992, the Forestry Canada Fire Danger Group documented the complete national edition of the Fire Behavior Prediction System, which followed an interim edition issued in 1984 (printed pp. 7, 8). Its preface named six developers, Van Wagner, Stocks, Lawson, Alexander, Lynham and McAlpine (printed p. 7). Its opening section said the Fire Weather Index (FWI) System was first introduced across Canada in 1971, and that its version then in use dated from 1984 (printed p. 8). The System covered 16 fuel types in five groups (printed p. 11). Table 1 listed 534 fire entries, 415 experimental and 119 wild or prescribed, drawn from 495 original observations (printed p. 10). Of these entries, 493 served the rate of spread models and 289 the fuel consumption models, and the grass type rested on Australian data (printed p. 10). Every rate of spread equation took the Initial Spread Index (ISI) as its only independent variable (printed p. 24). Its weather inputs were wind speed and direction and the Fine Fuel Moisture Code (FFMC) of the FWI System, and the Buildup Index adjusted the spread rate (printed pp. 10, 16). Equation 52 gave ISI = 0.208 × f(W) × f(F), and equation 53 gave f(W) = e^(0.05039 × WSV) (printed p. 32). WSV was the net effective wind speed in km/h, equal to the observed wind on level ground, and f(F) came from equations 45 and 46 (printed pp. 31, 32, 44, 61). Equation 46 printed its moisture constant as 147.2 (printed p. 31). Footnote 2 added equation 53a, f(W) = 12 × [1 − e^(−0.0818 × (WSV − 28))], under the printed condition If WS > 40, where WS was the observed wind speed (printed pp. 32, 61). The sentence beneath it applied 53a when the wind speed vector exceeded 40 km/h, and Appendix I repeated both branches (printed pp. 32, 56). The footnote presented 53a as a change to the ISI used in the FBP System (printed p. 32). The report also kept that ISI apart from the standard FWI System ISI, because it included slope (printed p. 33). Evaluated by wind_function_check.py in this folder, equations 53 and 53a gave 7.505 and 7.503 at 40 km/h, and 20.6 and 11.1 at 60 km/h. Equation 53a approached 12 and never exceeded it, so it limited the wind term above 40 km/h. The April 1994 errata sheet listed corrections to equations 32, 77 and 80 and none to equations 52, 53 or 53a (PDF p. 8). Section 5.0 set out three options for computing the FFMC and ISI for the System, drawn as Figure 2 (printed pp. 16 to 18). Option 1 took the standard daily FFMC from noon temperature, relative humidity, wind speed and precipitation, with a noon or afternoon wind for the ISI (printed pp. 17, 18). Option 2 read the diurnal table of Van Wagner (1972) for 12:00 to 20:00 without further weather data (printed pp. 17, 18). Option 3 ran the hourly FFMC program on actual or forecast hourly temperature, relative humidity, wind speed and rainfall (printed pp. 17, 18). The report judged the hourly method preferable for fire behaviour prediction and named the hourly FFMC the "true" reference value (printed p. 17). It said the daily minimum of fine fuel moisture could fall as late as 18:00 to 20:00, yet kept 16:00 as the traditional peak of fire danger (printed p. 17). It described the FFMC time lag as about one day of normal weather and the FWI System wind speed as an average (printed pp. 33, 35).

Bears on

- Introduction, "Fire danger in Canada was rated with the Canadian Forest Fire Weather Index System from 1970" (qmd L2188). Qualifies it, because the report dated the System's introduction across the country to 1971, a year after the first edition the sentence cites (printed p. 8).
- Introduction, "It is the part of the Canadian Forest Fire Danger Rating System that models fuel moisture and fire behaviour from weather alone" (qmd L2188). Adds to it, because the report called the FWI System the first completed subsystem, giving "relative measures of fuel moisture and fire behavior potential" (printed p. 8).
- Introduction, "From one observation a day, taken at 13:00 local daylight time" (qmd L2188). Adds to it, because the report said the standard daily FFMC was calculated from noon LST weather observations, which is 13:00 under daylight time (printed p. 17).
- Introduction, "In 2025 the Canadian Forest Service Fire Danger Group replaced the System's 1984 version" (qmd L2188). Adds to it, because the report said the version in use in 1992 dated from 1984, citing Van Wagner 1987 and Van Wagner and Pickett 1985 (printed p. 8).
- Introduction, "An hourly method for the Fine Fuel Moisture Code had existed since" (qmd L2188). Adds to it, because the report named Van Wagner (1977a) as that method, listed its four hourly inputs and said it was under revision in 1992 (printed pp. 16, 17).
- Introduction, "The report stated that hourly calculation allowed it to" (qmd L2188). Adds to it, because the 1992 report already stated that noon-based daily and hourly FFMC values would differ, and made the hourly value the reference (printed p. 17).
- Introduction, "The diurnally adjusted code came from look-up tables" (qmd L2188). Qualifies it, because the diurnal table the 1992 report offered covered only 12:00 to 20:00 and assumed a standard afternoon weather curve (printed pp. 16, 17).
- Introduction, "That function "limits the impact of high wind speeds on the ISI at wind speeds over 40km/h" (qmd L2188). Adds to it, because equation 53a of 1992 applied above 40 km/h and could not exceed 12 (printed p. 32).
- Introduction, "The hourly noon values were biased low because" (qmd L2192). Adds to it, because the 1992 report already said the minimum fine fuel moisture could fall as late as 18:00 to 20:00, and kept the peak of danger at 16:00 (printed p. 17).
- Introduction, "Wind speed entered the Initial Spread Index directly." (qmd L2194). Adds to it, because the report stated that FWI System wind speed was an average with no measure of its variability, about which real wind varied greatly (printed p. 35).
- Introduction, "FWI1987 on its 13:00 observation against FWI2025 on hourly weather would confound the version change with the input change." (qmd L2200). Adds to it, because the 1992 report already expected the noon-based daily FFMC and the hourly FFMC to differ, and took the hourly value as true (printed p. 17).
- Introduction, "Positive trend; little difference between series, because the new wind function acts only at 40 km h^-1^ and above" (qmd L2212). Qualifies it, because the report applied the new function only when wind was "greater than 40 km/h" (printed p. 32), and both branches gave about 7.5 at 40 km/h by wind_function_check.py.
- Methods, "The report judged the change "primarily relevant for extreme fire conditions"" (qmd L2466). Adds to it, because the 1992 report likewise described its change as a slight modification "at the extreme end" (printed p. 32).
