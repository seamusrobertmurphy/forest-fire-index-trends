Subject. How the Canadian fire growth model Prometheus built hourly weather and hourly Fire Weather Index System values from hourly or daily weather.

Establishes. Prometheus needed a continuous hourly stream of System values. Daily codes came from the 12:00 LST observation, and hourly FFMC, ISI and FWI from hourly weather, while the DMC, DC and BUI stayed daily. Daily extremes were expanded by the sine and exponential method of Beck and Trevitt (1989), with daily rain spread at 1/24 per hour. Default temperature offsets put the minimum 0.77 h before sunrise and the maximum 3.00 h after solar noon, with decay −2.20. Wind defaults were 1.00, 1.24 and −3.59. They came from Beck's 39 stations, with seasonal effects not examined. Two hourly FFMC methods were offered. Van Wagner (1977) answered rain at once but had a small day to night range, which overstated night growth in dry spells. Lawson and others (1996) swung more widely but ignored rain until the next noon. Subhourly steps used 147.27723 in both conversion equations to remove a small systematic bias. The text evaluated no method against measured fuel moisture or fire activity.

Equations 8 and 9 as printed on PDF p. 45, read from the image, were MC = 147.27723 · (101 − HFFMCin) / (59.5 + HFFMCin) and HFFMCout = 59.5 · (250 − m) / (147.27723 + m).

Bears on

- Introduction, "An hourly method for the Fine Fuel Moisture Code had existed since @vanwagner1977hourly, and a sinusoidal method for expanding daily extremes" (qmd L2188). Supports it. By 2010 Prometheus ran both, and the 2025 report adapted its Beck and Trevitt method (GLC-X-42 PDF pp. 15, 46, printed pp. 9, 40).
- Introduction, "eliminate the differences that arise in the values of the daily FFMC, the hourly FFMC and the diurnally adjusted FFMC" (qmd L2188). Adds to it. Tymstra described those differences in 2010, with the Lawson code swinging more between day and night than Van Wagner's and ignoring rain until noon.
- Introduction, "the time lag function used in the FWI2025 for the FFMC has been cut in half" (qmd L2188), with gap table R8 at L2465. Adds to it. In 2010 the small day to night range of Van Wagner's hourly code was already judged to overstate night fire growth. The text did not name the time lag as the cause.
- Introduction, "The Duff Moisture Code and the Drought Code were recalculated hourly, which the report judged 'may be more granular than necessary'" (qmd L2188). Supports it. Prometheus held both codes daily, assuming they changed "little from hour to hour".
- Methods, Study Design, "Under pathway C the 13:00 observation was converted to estimated daily maxima and minima" and "Those extremes were expanded to an hourly stream by the sinusoidal method of @beck1989diurnal" (qmd L2475), with gap table C9 and R14. Adds to it. The 2025 Table A-1 defaults (temperature 0, 2.75, −1.9; wind 1.0, 1.5, −1.3) replaced the Prometheus defaults above. Rain moved from 1/24 per hour to the hour after sunrise.
- Methods, Study Design, the same sentences (qmd L2475). Adds a limit, by inference. Prometheus's sine method did not model fronts, gusts or night burning. The 2025 report kept "a simple model of sinusoidal variation" (GLC-X-42 PDF p. 46, printed p. 40), so S2 likely shared that limit.
- Methods, Index Computation, "The implementation took 147.2772, which is $59.5 \times 250 / 101$" (qmd L2565). Adds a precedent, with a caveat. Tymstra printed 147.27723 in both conversions to remove a bias that accumulated over repeated steps. It kept the original constant for hourly and daily values, which the revised one underestimated.
- Methods, Seasonal Metrics, "The FWI2025 series were summarised to a daily value at 16:00 Local Standard Time" (qmd L2569). Supports it. Prometheus set the daily code's target at 1600 LST because the noon-based code "represents the potential fire behavior at that time".
- Same sentence (qmd L2569), checked in the primary source on 2026-11-06. Van Wagner (1977) tuned the hourly rates by trial and error against the standard code, settling on 1/8 of the daily rates (PDF p. 4, printed p. 2). It called 1600 hr "the traditional time of day for the correlation of fuel MC and fire behaviour in Canadian fire research" (PDF p. 8, printed p. 6). It judged "the conventional l600-hr is probably still a fair choice as time of maximum fire danger" (PDF p. 9, printed p. 7, with l for 1 as printed in the layer).
