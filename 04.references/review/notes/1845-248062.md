Subject. The fourth edition of the operational tables of the Fire Weather Index System, with the observing standards, start and end rules and the 1984 continuity claim for its revised Fine Fuel Moisture Code and Initial Spread Index.

Establishes. The text was the table form of the System as it stood in 1984, the edition before the 1987 equations. Its front matter set the daily observation at noon, defined as 1200 standard time or 1300 daylight time, taken even in rain. It specified dry and wet bulb temperature to 0.5 degrees Celsius, wind averaged over at least 10 minutes from a cup anemometer preferably 10 m above ground in the open, and rain from noon to noon to 0.2 mm. Calculations began on the third day after snow had essentially left, or on the third successive day with noon temperature of 12 degrees Celsius or higher, at FFMC 85, DMC 6 and DC 15, and ran until snow covered the ground. Missed days were to be estimated, never left blank. The foreword stated that the edition changed the FFMC and ISI equations but that outputs remained almost identical, so that statistical continuity with earlier records was preserved. The rain tables took effect at 0.6 mm for the FFMC, 1.5 mm for the DMC and 2.9 mm for the DC. The text evaluated nothing and the numeric tables were not read.

Bears on

- Introduction, "From one observation a day, taken at 13:00 local daylight time" (qmd L2186-2225). Supports it. The 1984 tables defined noon as 1200 standard time or 1300 daylight time, the first of the texts in this batch to fix a clock hour rather than solar noon.
- Introduction, "Their rain thresholds of 1.5 mm and 2.8 mm were applied to rain accumulated within a rain event" (qmd L2186-2225). Supports it. The 1984 rain tables took effect at 1.5 mm for the DMC and 2.9 mm for the DC, so rain of 2.8 mm or less left the DC unchanged, consistent with a threshold that rain must exceed 2.8 mm.
- Introduction, "The report's section 4.1 nonetheless claimed continuity" (qmd L2186-2225). Adds a precedent. The 1984 foreword made the same kind of claim for its FFMC and ISI changes, that outputs remained almost identical and statistical continuity with earlier records was preserved, without an evaluation printed beside it.
- Fire Danger Systems, "Its inputs were air temperature, relative humidity, wind speed at ten metres in the open and rainfall over the previous 24 hours" (qmd L2459-2468). Supports it. The 1984 standard was a 10 minute mean wind from a cup anemometer preferably at 10 m in the open and rain from noon to noon.
- Fire Danger Systems, "Each code was updated daily from its previous value with no reference period" (qmd L2459-2468). Supports it. The System required an unbroken daily record and estimates for every missed day.
- Methods, "The window was fixed by calendar in every season, 1 May to 30 September, 153 days, with the calculation started on 1 April. It replaced the Canadian start rule, which began the calculation three days after the snow cover disappeared" (qmd L2551). Supports it and adds the 1984 startup values, FFMC 85, DMC 6 and DC 15, the 12 degree rule for snow free regions, and the end rule, until snow covered the ground.
- Study Design, "All three ran on the same weather, season window, startup values and gap filling" (qmd L2469-2476). Adds a precedent. The 1984 instructions filled missed days from on site recorders, nearby stations, the mean of the adjacent days or the recent weather pattern.
- Index Computation, "The coefficient that converted the Fine Fuel Moisture Code to a moisture content was printed as 147.2 by @vanwagner1987fwi" (qmd L2561-2566). Adds to it. The 1984 foreword said the equations, not the tables, were the primary form of the System and pointed to the Van Wagner and Pickett (1984) Fortran program for the purest mathematical output.
