Subject. The first evaluation of the Fire Weather Index against observed fire occurrence in Ontario, with its class breakdown and a comparison with Turner's British Columbia calibration.

Establishes. Daily weather from 85 Department of Lands and Forests stations over 1963 to 1968, from the start of each fire season to 30 September, was run through Simard's 1970 program, giving 79,938 station days. Each of 3,159 forest fires, grass fires excluded, was matched to the nearest station. Relative fire occurrence, fires per occurrence of an index value divided by the value on an average day, rose steadily and nearly linearly with the index, doubling when the Fire Weather Index doubled. Days at 41 and above had 35 times the occurrence of days at 0. The Adjusted Duff Moisture Code at 101 and above had 46 times the occurrence of 2 to 3. The Fine Fuel Moisture Code curve was the only curved one, flat below 70 to 80 and steep above. Classes were set so that 2 per cent of days, about three a season, were Extreme, giving Very Low 0, Low 1 to 3, Moderate 4 to 10, High 11 to 22 and Extreme 23 and above. Turner's British Columbia study, 20 stations and 16,568 fires over ten years, gave the same linear form with half the Ontario slope. Fires were not split by cause.

Bears on

- Introduction, "Fire danger has been rated throughout Canada since 1970" (qmd L2186-2225). Supports it and adds that the index was provisionally introduced in 1969.
- Introduction, "A danger rating system was judged on whether its outputs tracked fire occurrence" (qmd L2186-2225). Supports it with the first Canadian test, 85 stations, 79,938 station days and 3,159 fires over 1963 to 1968, each fire matched to the nearest station, and a relative occurrence that doubled when the Fire Weather Index doubled.
- Introduction, "@andrews2003evaluation set out percentile analysis and logistic regression of fire days on a danger index" (qmd L2186-2225). Adds to it. The ratio of the share of fires to the share of days in a class, the relative fire occurrence, was in Canadian use by 1971.
- Introduction, "In British Columbia over 1981 to 2000, daily fire occurrence was modelled against danger" (qmd L2186-2225). Adds to it. The earliest British Columbia calibration was Turner (1970), 20 stations and 16,568 fires over ten years, with the same linear form as Ontario at about half the slope.
- Methods, "Absolute thresholds applied unchanged to all three series. A Fine Fuel Moisture Code of 91 was taken from section 4.1" (qmd L2571). Adds to it. In Ontario 1963 to 1968 relative fire occurrence at a Fine Fuel Moisture Code of 91 to 92 was 5.4 times the average day and the curve turned steep above 70 to 80, and the Ontario Extreme class began at a Fire Weather Index of 23, set as the top 2 per cent of days, not at 30.
- Table of expectations, "Daily lightning, person-caused and all-cause ignitions | Positive association with daily danger" (qmd L2186-2225). Supports the positive association for all causes together, but the text did not separate lightning from person caused fires.
- Introduction, "The Fine Fuel Moisture Code (FFMC) tracks the surface litter" and the Drought Code (qmd L2186-2225). Adds to it. Against fire occurrence the Drought Code had the lowest slope and widest scatter of the six components.
